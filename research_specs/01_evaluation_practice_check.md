# Evaluation Practice Check: Do LLM-Team Coordination Papers Measure Against a Computed Optimum?

Date: 2026-09-08
Method: web search (arXiv/OpenReview/ACL/AAMAS coverage), then direct HTML fetch + text-grep of primary sources for load-bearing quotes. Every quote below was pulled from the arXiv HTML rendering of the cited paper (version noted), not from a search-engine summary.

## Verdict

**Partially closed.** The broad claim as stated - "no one measures LLM team coordination against a formally computed optimal value" - is false: at least two live subfields (LLM negotiation vs. Nash/Pareto solutions, and LLM multi-agent task/decision problems vs. integer-programming-computed optima) routinely compute an exact optimum and report a normalized gap to it. But the narrower and more defensible version of the claim survives intact: in every case found, the computed "optimum" is defined over a **centralized, full-information policy class** (an omniscient solver with access to the pooled state, or to both parties' utility functions) - never over the **decentralized policy class** that the LLM team actually operates under (bounded per-agent observation, communication cost, no shared memory). No paper was found that computes an exact or even provably near-optimal decentralized policy (the Dec-POMDP-optimal object) and reports LLM-team distance to it. So: the "against no optimum at all" version of the claim is refuted; the "against no *decentralized* optimum" version is not refuted by anything found, and is consistent with Dec-POMDP being NEXP-hard, so nobody centrally computes that object for any benchmark-scale task, LLM or not. A proposal built on the strict "no optimum ever" framing dies; a proposal built on "no *decentralized*-optimum ceiling" survives this search.

## On a decentralized-policy computed optimum (the single most important question)

**None found.** Searched directly for Dec-POMDP-optimal LLM-team evaluations, "optimal decentralized policy" + LLM, and MILP-for-Dec-POMDP applied to LLM agents. The Dec-POMDP theory papers found (e.g., arXiv 2604.09495, MIT survey by Amato et al.) confirm why: exact Dec-POMDP solution is NEXP-complete in the number of agents, and even a locally optimal solution is NP-hard, so no one - LLM literature or classical MARL literature - computes an exact decentralized-policy optimum for any task of realistic size. The closest anyone gets (papers #1-4 above) computes the optimum *as if* the team had centralized access to the union of all private information, then measures how far the decentralized (partial-info, no-shared-memory) LLM team falls short of that centralized bound. That gap conflates two different sources of loss - (a) suboptimal use of one's own information and (b) the information loss inherent to decentralization itself - and none of the papers found separates them. That conflation is a real, defensible gap in the literature and is a substantially narrower, better-supported claim than "no one computes any optimum at all."

## What survives

1. **"No LLM-team evaluation compares against ANY formally computed optimum"** - refuted. Counterexamples #1-4 above are real, quote-verified, and load-bearing (not decorative).
2. **"No LLM-team evaluation compares against an EXACT DECENTRALIZED-policy optimum"** - not refuted by anything found. Every computed "optimum" in the literature is a centralized-information relaxation (full state, full information union, or both parties' true utility functions), used as an upper bound for a genuinely decentralized team. No paper decomposes the resulting gap into "loss from decentralization" vs. "loss from poor reasoning given what the team does know." This is a real, narrower, and much more attackable gap than the original claim - and it's consistent with what Dec-POMDP complexity theory predicts should be true (nobody computes that object for anything, LLM or not, because it's intractable).
3. The distribution is heavily weighted toward trained-MARL baseline, human baseline, or "no ceiling / rubric" (see table) - this part of the underlying intuition behind the original claim is directionally right even though "none ever" is not.
4. Single-agent LLM-as-optimizer work (TSP/knapsack/VRP with OR-solver optimality gaps: HeuriGym, CO-Bench, TSPLIB-based papers, COMPASS) is a mature, large subfield that already reports normalized optimality gaps - but it is not a multi-agent *team* coordination claim, so it doesn't itself close the gap; it does mean any novelty argument resting on "LLM optimization work never compares to computed optima" would also be flatly wrong, separate from the team-coordination question.

## Verbatim null queries

- `"LLM agents compared to optimal decentralized policy Dec-POMDP solver gap coordination 2026"` → search engine's own synthesis: "the specific comparison of 'performance gap' between LLM agents and optimal Dec-POMDP solvers is not directly addressed in the retrieved papers... the search results don't contain specific quantitative comparisons or 'gap' metrics between LLM agent performance and optimal Dec-POMDP solutions as of 2026."
- `"LLM" agents "competitive ratio" OR "approximation ratio" multi-agent coordination evaluation` → no paper surfaced applying competitive-ratio or approximation-ratio framing to an LLM multi-agent team (single-agent online-algorithms framing exists elsewhere but not surfaced here for teams).
- `multi-agent LLM team traveling salesman OR knapsack OR vehicle routing gap to optimal solver benchmark 2025 2026` → returned only single-agent-as-optimizer papers (DRAGON, GOAL, COMPASS) and one non-LLM MARL/VRP paper (MAEnvs4VRP); no genuine multi-agent-LLM-team-vs-solver-optimum paper for TSP/knapsack/VRP found.
- `Melting Pot LLM agents evaluation benchmark cooperation` → only one 2024 paper (arXiv 2403.11381) applying Melting Pot to LLM agents was found; it uses focal-population generalization against trained MARL populations, no computed optimum.
