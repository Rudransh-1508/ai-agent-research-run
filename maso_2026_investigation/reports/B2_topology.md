## 1. Bottom line

**The dynamic-topology/orchestration landscape is large and almost entirely PERFORMANCE- and COST-driven; a negative result (MA-Evolve, arXiv:2609.04217) even shows multi-agent structure adds cost without clear benefit at equal inference budget. Robustness appears mostly in an adversarial-security frame (attack detection + graph intervention: G-Safeguard, FGLGuard, GAMMAF). Runtime restructuring *as a response to degradation/abnormality* now exists in three verified 2026 works — ATM (overload-triggered agent factorisation), DynaGraph (confidence-triggered subgraph reconstruction as "self-healing"), MASFly (watcher-triggered reconfiguration on abnormal behavior) — but none measures blast radius/systemic impact, none models organizational boundaries (authority, accountability, human-agent), none studies containment-vs-utility trade-offs for organic faults, and triggers are shallow heuristics (overload index, execution confidence, abnormality watch). The seed hypothesis is NOT killed — but it must differentiate from this 2026 restructuring cluster, from Shen et al. EMNLP 2025 (design-time topology for error suppression with utility preservation), and from MAS-FIRE (fault injection showing topology passively determines fault tolerance).**

Three sub-findings:

1. **What they optimize:** every topology-design method verified (GPT-Swarm, DyLAN, MacNet, AgentPrune, G-Designer, ADAS, AFlow, MaAS, MAS-ZERO, ScoreFlow, AgentSquare, AMAS, AgentConductor, TopoPrior, HELENA, EvoAgent, AgentVerse, DyTopo, MAS-GPT, SwarmAgentic, ReActNet) optimizes task accuracy and/or token/$ cost. None optimizes a robustness, containment, or recovery objective. Robustness claims, where present (AgentPrune, G-Designer), are *evaluation add-ons* under adversarial attack, not design objectives. MA-Evolve (2609.04217) is a cautionary null: at equal LLM-call budget, an evolved Planner-Executor-Critic team is not statistically better than an evolved single agent (p=0.80).
2. **Fault containment via restructuring:** two clusters. (a) *Adversarial*: G-Safeguard (ACL 2025; GNN anomaly detection + topological intervention), FGLGuard (2609.02967, federated), GAMMAF (2604.24477, benchmark with live node isolation). (b) *Non-adversarial degradation*: ATM (2607.20488, overload-triggered factorisation with safety invariants), DynaGraph (2605.29511, confidence-triggered fine-grained patching + subgraph reconstruction for "severe logical ruptures", explicitly motivated by static topologies' vulnerability to cascading errors), MASFly (2602.13671, Watcher agent reconfigures the system upon abnormal behavior using prior failure experience). Cluster (b) is the seed's nearest neighbor: restructuring-as-response exists, but as performance engineering with heuristic triggers — no blast-radius metric, no propagation model across boundary types, no organizational semantics, no utility-budgeted containment.
3. **What determines spread:** Shen et al. (EMNLP 2025 main) publish a causal account of error propagation vs topology sparsity and a design-time learner (EIB-Learner) balancing error suppression against beneficial diffusion — containment-with-utility on the communication dimension is occupied at design time. MAS-FIRE (Feb 2026) shows iterative closed-loop topologies neutralize >40% of faults that collapse linear ones. The Byzantine-consensus line (CP-WBFT 2511.10400; DecentLLMs 2507.14928; SAC 2605.09076 EMNLP 2026; Resilient Consensus 2606.15024; Semantic Raft IWQoS 2026) imports classical (F+1)-robustness / W-MSR / Raft machinery into LLM MAS. A MASO paper must position against all of these.

**Direct answer to the assignment question:** Runtime restructuring *as a response to degradation or abnormality* is **claimed** (ATM, DynaGraph, MASFly; adversarial variant: G-Safeguard line) — the seed is not alone there. What remains unclaimed: restructuring as **fault containment and recovery measured by systemic impact** — i.e., (a) a propagation/blast-radius model spanning boundary types beyond communication (role, authority/delegation, memory, accountability, human-agent); (b) containment/recovery mechanisms evaluated on systemic-impact-vs-task-utility trade-offs; (c) organic/correlated faults rather than attacks or overload; (d) recovery of correct system state, not just performance rescue. The *determinants* of spread on the communication graph (Shen et al., NetSafe, MAS-FIRE, SAC, Resilient Consensus, CP-WBFT) and *evaluation* of propagation (MAS-FIRE, Containing the Cascade, SCHEMA) are published; the 2026 restructuring cluster provides neither the measurement nor the organizational semantics.

## 6. Kill/subsume analysis for the seed hypothesis

**Does anything here kill the seed?** No. **Does anything partially subsume it?** Yes, five clusters:

1. **Shen et al. EMNLP 2025** subsumes "topology determines error spread" + "design topology to suppress errors while preserving utility" — *on the communication boundary only, at design time*. The seed must either go beyond communication (role/authority/memory/accountability/human boundaries) or beyond design-time (runtime containment/recovery), and cite this head-on.
2. **MAS-FIRE (2602.19843)** subsumes the *experimental method* (fault taxonomy + injection + recovery-behavior tiers + topology as variable). A seed paper should adopt/extend this template, not present it as new.
3. **G-Safeguard → FGLGuard / GAMMAF** subsumes "graph intervention as containment" — *adversarial only*. Seed must differentiate: organic faults, state recovery, utility budget, organizational semantics.
4. **The 2026 runtime-restructuring cluster (ATM 2607.20488; DynaGraph 2605.29511; MASFly 2602.13671)** subsumes "restructure the system at runtime when something degrades" as a *mechanism category* — but with heuristic triggers (overload index, execution confidence, abnormality watch), no propagation or blast-radius model, no organizational boundaries, no containment-vs-utility analysis, and (for ATM/DynaGraph) weak validation (unrefereed preprint; adapter-based "agents"). The seed must cite all three and show what the organizational, measurement-first framing adds.
5. **The Byzantine/consensus line (CP-WBFT 2511.10400; DecentLLMs 2507.14928; SAC 2605.09076; Resilient Consensus 2606.15024; Semantic Raft IWQoS 2026; FAIR-Swarm LaMAS 2026)** subsumes "classical fault-tolerance machinery imported into LLM MAS" for agreement-style tasks. The seed's organizational workflows (delegation, accountability, human handoffs) are not consensus tasks — differentiation is natural but must be argued.

Also note the adversarial pressure from **MA-Evolve (2609.04217)**: if multi-agent structure itself does not beat a single agent at equal compute, a seed paper must justify the multi-agent organizational setting on grounds other than raw benchmark gains (e.g., organizational realism, fault containment value).

**Residual defensible core for MASO (consistent with B1/E3):** runtime containment/recovery of *non-adversarial organizational* faults via restructuring, measured by an organizational blast-radius/utility trade-off, across boundaries the communication-graph literature does not model (authority/delegation, accountability, human-agent decision rights, cross-organization memory). Note the sibling MASO proposal occupies delegation invariants + causal accountability across A2A/MCP — the seed must stay on the *failure-propagation/recovery* side of that line.
