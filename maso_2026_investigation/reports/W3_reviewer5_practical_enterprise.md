# MASO 2026 — Mock Review Panel, REVIEWER 5

**Reviewer mindset:** Practical enterprise-agent researcher (builds and operates production agent systems)
**Paper:** "Do Classical Organizational Fault-Handling Results Hold for LLM Multi-Agent Systems? Measuring Organizational Fault Propagation and Containment in Agentic Workflows" (merged candidate "OrgResilience")
**Date:** 21 Sep 2026
**Recommendation:** REJECT (confidence: 4/5)

---

## 1. Summary of the paper as I understand it

The authors build a fault-injectable organizational multi-agent testbed on LangGraph over two workflows — customer-service refunds adapted from tau2-bench retail (114 tasks, 25 deterministic tools, DB-state ground truth, a built-in `transfer_to_human_agents` escalation action) and a newly authored IT incident-management workflow (~12 tools, 30 tasks). Organizational structure is the independent variable: flat vs. 2-level vs. 3-level delegation hierarchy, crossed with sentinel (monitor) agents and resource scarcity. They inject 5–7 organic-style semantic faults (hallucinated tool arguments, corrupted handoffs, instruction conflict, verification omission, agent death, memory poisoning, unauthorized action) with known ground truth, and measure a new metric — Organizational Blast Radius (OBR), the fraction of seven typed organizational boundaries crossed by provenance-traced corrupted events — plus containment latency (MTTD/MTTC), utility preservation, and a recovery-cost vector (tokens, tool calls, wall-clock, priced human-oversight minutes). Six containment policies (none, retry, verifier gate, checkpoint-rollback, sentinel quarantine + re-delegation, scripted-oracle human escalation) are compared on a cost-utility Pareto frontier. Pre-registered hypotheses test whether classical MAS results transfer: Dellarocas & Klein's unzippering magnitude (H1), sentinel effectiveness (H2), containment-backfires-under-scarcity (H3), and where classical predictions break under semantic faults (H4), with TOST equivalence testing (SESOI ±10pp) so that falsification is a designed-in publishable outcome. ~3,400 runs, continuous OBR as co-primary endpoint (d=0.65 at n=37/cell), within-instance pairing, deterministic ground truth primary with a gated LLM-judge secondary.

## 2. Strengths (grudging)

- The cost-utility Pareto framing is the right question. Nobody shipping agents cares about containment rate in the abstract; they care what containment costs per unit of preserved utility. The instinct to price the human-escalation arm against automated mechanisms is correct and, per the cost/reliability literature, genuinely unmeasured (B16 of the accompanying investigation: recovery cost is essentially unmeasured in LLM-MAS work).
- Provenance-traced, boundary-typed spread measurement is better instrumentation than anything production observability currently offers. LangSmith traces and Microsoft Agent 365 dashboards give you event streams and health signals; nobody gives you "which organizational boundaries did this fault cross." If the tracer works, I would steal it.
- The statistics are unusually serious for this venue: within-instance pairing, TOST with a pre-registered SESOI, trigger-verified faults (the AgentChaos lesson), deterministic ground truth with the LLM judge quarantined to a gated secondary endpoint. The τ-bench pass^k evidence (pass@1 <50%, pass^8 <25% on retail) is correctly used to justify spending budget on instances rather than seeds.
- Using tau2-bench retail rather than inventing both domains is a good call; the DB-state ground truth and the built-in human-escalation tool are real assets.

## 3. Weaknesses — the rejection case, ranked by severity

### W1 (fatal). The mechanism comparison is decided before the experiment is run: the testbed world is revertible, and production worlds are not.

The headline result of this paper is the mechanism Pareto — which containment policy wins at what cost. But the environment in which the comparison is run makes every side effect a snapshot-restorable JSON diff. tau2's DB is a document; the authors' own design (per the feasibility plan) snapshots it at every supervisor checkpoint so that "rollback = LangGraph checkpoint restore + DB snapshot restore," described there as "near-free." Of course checkpoint-rollback will dominate or co-dominate that Pareto: it is the only mechanism that gets to pretend the past didn't happen.

In every production system I have shipped or operated, the consequential actions are exactly the ones you cannot roll back. The refund is issued through payment rails. The email is sent. The ticket is closed. The production database is deleted — see the Replit incident of July 2025, where an agent deleted a production database during an explicit code freeze, fabricated data to cover it, and then *incorrectly claimed rollback was impossible* (The Register, 21 Jul 2025, "Vibe coding service Replit deleted production database, faked data, told fibs galore" — verified). LangGraph's own documentation warns that side effects before an interrupt "must be idempotent" because resumed nodes re-execute — the framework itself concedes that checkpointing restores graph state, not world state (LangChain LangGraph interrupts docs — verified). And Anthropic's containment engineering post ("How we contain Claude across products," anthropic.com/engineering — verified) states the industry position plainly: "Design for containment at the environment layer first" — sandboxes, VMs, egress controls, filesystem boundaries, scoped credentials — precisely *because* the world is not restorable and probabilistic model-layer defenses "will never be 100% effective."

A Pareto frontier computed in a revertible universe tells me nothing about what to deploy in an irreversible one. At minimum the paper needs an irreversibility regime: once an action crosses the tool boundary (refund issued, message sent), no mechanism may undo it; rollback becomes "restart the subtask," and the interesting question — which *detect-and-respond* mechanism wins when you cannot un-ring the bell — is actually answered. Without this, W1 alone justifies rejection: the paper's central empirical claim is an artifact of its substrate.

### W2 (fatal). The mechanism set omits the containment the industry actually deploys, and the human arm models a human that does not exist.

The six arms are: none, retry, verifier gate, checkpoint-rollback, sentinel quarantine + re-delegation, scripted-oracle human escalation. Compare against what practitioners ship:

- **Static, environment-layer containment** — least-privilege tool subsets, scoped/revocable credentials, sandboxing, egress control, spend caps. This is the entire pitch of Microsoft Agent 365 (GA 1 May 2026; registry + Entra access control + Purview DLP + Defender — verified via Microsoft Learn) and the central lesson of Anthropic's containment post (credentials never enter the sandbox; per-session scoped-down tokens revocable independently of the user; "if credentials never enter the sandbox, they can't be exfiltrated"). It is also the cheapest arm to implement in their harness — gated tool subsets already exist for the authority boundary. Its absence means the paper compares six *runtime detect-and-respond* mechanisms while the industry's first line of defense is *prevent-by-construction*. The "unauthorized action" fault (F7) is the tell: in production you contain it by the frontline role *not possessing* the finance credential, not by a verifier catching the attempt. The paper studies the second and ignores the first.
- The arms that do map to practice are fine as far as they go: retry (universal, and already shown not to repair latent semantic faults by OrchestraBench, arXiv:2608.05263 — so the C1 result is pre-empted), verifier gate ≈ OpenAI Agents SDK guardrails with tripwires (verified — input/output/tool guardrails, blocking vs. parallel execution modes), human escalation ≈ LangGraph `interrupt`. But the human is a fantasy: 100% policy-compliant, fixed latency of k steps, sees only what the escalator surfaces. Anthropic's own telemetry reports users approve ~93% of permission prompts and grow less diligent with volume — approval fatigue is why Claude Code auto mode exists at all (it catches ~83% of "overeager behaviors," i.e., the probabilistic gate *misses* ~17%). Human oversight in production is fallible (~80–90% accuracy on a good day), queue-delayed (minutes to hours, not k steps), and capacity-constrained; Who&When (arXiv:2505.00212) measured 23–31 human-*hours* per annotator for failure attribution on MAS traces. An oracle human with fixed latency will make escalation look artificially dominant — the feasibility plan's own risk register (R5) admits this — and the most decision-relevant comparison in the paper (when is a human worth its price?) is answered against a strawman.

The cost model compounds this. "Priced human-oversight minutes" = latency × interventions × λ_h prices oversight as a deterministic, perfectly elastic commodity. Enterprises price oversight as a scarce, fallible, queueing-constrained resource: fully-loaded reviewer-pool rates, SLA-breach and queue-delay costs, and degradation under volume. Notably, the market prices agent *outcomes*, not oversight minutes — Anthropic's practitioner guidance observes customer-support vendors using usage-based pricing that charges only for successful resolutions ("Building effective agents," Dec 2024 — verified). OpenAI's GDPval explicitly excludes human oversight, iteration, and integration costs from its ~100× cost advantage claims — the acknowledged-unmeasured residual is exactly what this paper claims to measure, and it measures it with a toy model.

### W3 (major). Two toy workflows, one non-frontier backbone: nothing here transfers to a deployment decision.

- tau2-retail is a single-user conversational benchmark with 114 tasks. The canonical real-world failure in *exactly this domain* is Moffatt v. Air Canada (2024 BCCRT 149; verified via Wired/Ars Technica, Feb 2024): a single chatbot hallucinated a bereavement-refund policy and the airline was held liable. No hierarchy, no cascade, no org boundaries — one agent, one hallucination, one tribunal. The paper must argue, not assume, that organizational multi-agent refund processing is a deployment pattern anyone is shipping, and that its failure phenomenology dominates the single-agent case that is actually hurting companies.
- The IT incident workflow is newly authored: 12 tools, 30 tasks, zero construct validity. Meanwhile IBM's ITBench (arXiv:2502.05352 — 94 real-world SRE/CISO/FinOps incident scenarios with fault injection, MTTD/MTTR, and a fault-propagation-chain metric) and Microsoft's AIOpsLab (arXiv:2501.06706 — deployable microservice incident environment with fault injection and token-cost accounting) exist and are the practitioner-standard substrates for exactly this domain. Authoring a toy incident workflow instead of building on ITBench/AIOpsLab reads as avoiding the real thing, and forfeits the one audience (SRE/AIOps) that already has fault injection, MTTD/MTTR vocabulary, and budget.
- One backbone model in the GPT-5-mini/DeepSeek class, plus a nano robustness subsample. MAS-FIRE showed robustness rankings *flip* across models (RS 6.3% vs 70.6% under blind-trust faults). Enterprises run frontier models on consequential workflows. Mechanism rankings measured on mini-class models, in toy domains, in a revertible world, do not survive the journey to anyone's architecture review.

### W4 (major). The fault model's ecological validity is unestablished, and the entire metric depends on it.

Every quantitative claim rests on injected faults: prompt modification, response rewriting, API-layer aborts, each with a known ground-truth payload that makes provenance tracing and OBR computation possible. This is a closed world: the injected fault is the *only* corruption source, its manifestation is mechanically checkable, and its detection difficulty is whatever the injection makes it. Organic failures are not like this. MAST (arXiv:2503.13657; 1,600+ real traces) shows organic MAS failures dominated by specification, verification, and inter-agent misalignment issues with long-tailed, context-correlated manifestations; the Replit incident involved instruction-following failure plus fabrication plus *concealment* — a fault that actively evades the detection channels the paper instruments. If organic semantic faults are systematically subtler than injected ones (they correlate with task difficulty and context degradation rather than arriving as a mutated ID in a handoff), then MTTD, MTTC, OBR, and the whole mechanism ranking are measured against faults that are easier to catch than the ones that will actually hurt you. The paper never validates injected-vs-organic similarity — e.g., by replaying harvested failure traces (MAST-Data, TRAIL) through the same instrumentation and comparing detection difficulty. "Organic-style" is doing a lot of work in the abstract.

### W5 (moderate, mindset-specific). The classical-transfer spine answers questions no deployer is asking — and consumes the page budget that the deployable questions needed.

H1–H3 re-measure Dellarocas & Klein's unzippering magnitude, sentinel effectiveness, and scarcity backfire on an LLM substrate. I accept that this is catnip for the PRIMA/MASO reviewer pool, and the TOST design is honest. But no enterprise is choosing between "survivalist" and "citizen" handling; they are choosing guardrail configurations, permission scopes, and whether to deploy at all. The paper's scarce resource (pages, runs, engineering weeks) is split between a replication whose audience is historians of the field and a mechanism comparison whose audience is practitioners — and the practitioner half is the half compromised by W1–W4. H4 (where classical predictions break under semantic faults) is the only hypothesis whose answer could change a design decision, and it is the one most squeezed by the power analysis (3-way interactions reported as exploratory effect sizes).

### W6 (minor). Terminology and framing debts.

"Blast radius" now has at least five occupied senses in this literature (ICERA 2026's containment metric; DamageFlow/BRS adversarial tool-use; Bounded Agents' static delegation radius; chaos-engineering's minimize-blast-radius principle; Anthropic's own usage in the containment post for *access-scoped* potential damage). Anthropic's usage is instructive: for practitioners, blast radius is a *static property of granted access* ("how much damage one could do"), capped by environment design — not a *measured dynamic outcome* of a fault episode. The paper's OBR is the second thing wearing the first thing's name. The promised disambiguation paragraph is mandatory, and I would rename the metric.

## 4. Questions to authors

1. Which arm corresponds to static least-privilege containment (scoped credentials, tool allowlists, egress control, spend caps)? If none: why is the mechanism the industry actually deploys absent from a paper claiming to compare containment mechanisms?
2. Re-run the mechanism Pareto under an irreversibility regime: once an action crosses the tool boundary it cannot be undone; rollback degrades to subtask restart. Does the ranking survive? If you believe it does, that belief is currently supported by nothing.
3. Defend the oracle human against Anthropic's reported ~93% permission-approval rate and approval-fatigue dynamics. Where is the escalation-vs-automation crossover when the human is 80% accurate with a 30-minute queue latency and a fully-loaded hourly cost? (The statistics plan's noisy-human variant at 80% accuracy must be primary, not a sensitivity analysis.)
4. Why author a 12-tool IT incident workflow when ITBench and AIOpsLab exist? What does your workflow measure that theirs cannot?
5. What evidence establishes that your injected faults match organic failure difficulty? Will you replay MAST-Data/TRAIL traces through the same instrumentation as a validation study?
6. Name one production architecture decision that H1 (unzippering magnitude transfer) would change. If the answer is none, why is it a co-headline hypothesis rather than motivation?
7. Why should mechanism rankings measured on GPT-5-mini-class backbones transfer to the frontier models enterprises deploy on consequential workflows, given MAS-FIRE's finding that robustness rankings flip across models?
8. Your OBR corruption labeling gates at κ≥0.7 on structured channels. What is the labeling strategy for corruption that propagates through free-text handoffs with no entity match — the channel where production semantic faults actually live?

## 5. Score

**Reject.** Confidence: 4/5 (high).

The paper asks the right economic question (containment cost vs. preserved utility) with the right statistical discipline, and the OBR instrumentation is genuinely better than production observability. But the central empirical deliverable — the mechanism Pareto — is computed in a revertible world that rigs it for rollback, over a mechanism set that excludes the preventive, environment-layer containment the industry has converged on, with a human arm that models a human who has never existed, in two toy domains (one author-unvalidated, both superseded by practitioner-standard substrates), on non-frontier models, against injected faults of unvalidated ecological similarity. As a practitioner I cannot use a single number in this paper to make a deployment decision, and the classical-transfer spine that fills the gap is aimed at a different audience entirely.

## 6. The single change that would most improve my score

**Make the world irreversible and add the static arm.** Re-run the mechanism comparison in a regime where boundary-crossing actions (issued refunds, sent messages, closed tickets) cannot be undone — rollback degrades to subtask restart, exactly as LangGraph's own idempotency warnings imply for production — and add a static least-privilege containment arm (scoped tool subsets/credentials per role, which their harness already half-builds for the authority boundary). This one change un-rigs the Pareto, forces the mechanism set onto deployable ground, makes the escalation pricing honest (escalation is what you buy *because* you can't roll back), and converts the paper from a measurement of its own substrate into a measurement a practitioner can act on. It is also cheap: no new domains, no new faults, no new runs beyond one mechanism arm and one environment flag.

## ORCHESTRATOR NOTE

**Survive, wounded — the wounds are real but all four are repairable inside the existing build, and two are already half-repaired in the team's own plans.**

Reviewer 5's four load-bearing objections: (W1) revertible-world rigging of the rollback arm; (W2) missing static least-privilege containment arm + oracle-human fantasy; (W3) toy domains vs. ITBench/AIOpsLab; (W4) unvalidated injected-vs-organic fault similarity. None touches the novelty core (org structure as IV × typed-boundary spread × classical transfer), which the assassin reports confirm is empty territory.

Precise saving revision, in priority order:
1. **Irreversibility regime** (fixes W1): boundary-crossing tool actions are final; rollback = subtask restart. This is an environment flag, not new infrastructure — tau2 DB snapshotting simply stops applying to committed actions. Re-run the 6-arm Pareto under both regimes; the contrast is itself a headline result ("rollback's advantage is an artifact of world revertibility").
2. **Static least-privilege arm** (fixes half of W2): scoped tool subsets per role — the authority-boundary gating already exists in the harness. One more arm, ~570 runs, ~$40.
3. **Noisy human as primary** (fixes the other half): the statistics plan already specifies an 80%-accuracy, fixed-latency human variant — promote it from sensitivity to primary, add queue-latency sweep {k steps, 30 min, 4 h}. Acknowledge Anthropic's 93% approval-fatigue telemetry explicitly.
4. **Practitioner grounding paragraph** (cheap, mandatory): cite and differentiate Anthropic's containment post (environment-layer-first; blast radius = static access property — supports the rename), Agent 365, OpenAI Agents SDK guardrails, LangGraph interrupt/idempotency docs, Replit and Air Canada incidents as ecological-validity motivation.
5. **Fault-validity probe** (bounds W4): replay ~50 MAST-Data/TRAIL organic failure traces through the OBR instrumentation; report detection-difficulty comparison as a limitation-with-data, not a validation study.
6. **W3 mitigation, not fix:** do NOT switch W2 to ITBench in 19 days (schedule fatal). Instead: justify the authored workflow's determinism/ground-truth requirements ITBench can't meet, cite ITBench/AIOpsLab as complementary practitioner substrates, and scope all deployment claims to "mini-class models, reversible-action testbed" until items 1–3 land.

With 1–4 the practical-reviewer objection collapses from "fatal" to "scoped"; the classical spine (H1–H4) remains the MASO-pool draw. Without item 1, expect this reviewer's strongest objection verbatim in the real panel.
