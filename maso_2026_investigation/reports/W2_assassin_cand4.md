# W2 ASSASSIN — CAND-4 "RecoveryCost" (The Cost of Containment)

**Date:** 2026-09-21
**Mission:** Kill CAND-4: "first benchmark comparing containment/recovery mechanisms on the cost-utility Pareto (tokens, tool calls, wall-clock, human oversight minutes) in organizational workflows; extends cost-of-pass to cost-of-recovery; human escalation priced against automated mechanisms."
**Assigned search targets:** cost-utility Pareto benchmark for containment/recovery in LLM MAS; token/tool/human-time cost of recovery (AIOpsLab 2501.06706, ITBench 2502.05352, cost-of-pass 2504.13359, Efficient Agents 2508.02694); "recovery cost" as a metric anywhere in agentic evaluation; human oversight cost measurement (Oversight Has a Capacity 2606.08919, GDPval); SRE incident-cost methodology transferred to agents; Containing the Cascade and DamageFlow cost-metric depth.

**Wave-1 basis:** B16 (cost/reliability) established recovery cost is unmeasured in core LLM-MAS resilience work, with pockets in AIOps (episode tokens, MTTR) and diagnosis (Who&When hours). This wave-2 sweep builds on B16/B15/B14/B8/E2/E6 and adds eight new verified items they did not cover (Keel, ToolMaze RC detail, MTTR-A, READY, CodeRescue, EAS resilience metrics, FTDI depth, Cost-Utility Alignment framework).

## VERDICT: WOUNDED (not killed)

CAND-4's exact combination — **multi-agent organizational workflows × propagating injected faults × containment/recovery mechanism comparison × full cost vector including human oversight minutes × task-utility preservation** — remains unoccupied. But every *component* claim is now occupied somewhere, and three of CAND-4's likely framing claims are dead on arrival:

1. **"First to measure recovery cost (of agent failures)" — DEAD.** At least six verified works measure recovery cost in some form (§1).
2. **"First cost-utility comparison of recovery mechanisms" — DEAD for single-agent/single-orchestrator; occupied at the frontier level by Keel (Sep 2026) and CodeRescue (withdrawn), at the budget-sweep level by Self-Healing Orchestrators, and partially in MAS code-generation by FTDI.**
3. **"First to bring SRE recovery metrics to LLM MAS" — DEAD.** MTTR-A (2511.20663) explicitly adapts classical dependability MTTR/MTBF to LLM MAS with a LangGraph benchmark; ITBench measures MTTD/MTTR for SRE agents.
4. **"Human oversight cost is unmeasured" — DEAD as stated.** READY (2609.02095) measures human-oversight burden + operating cost + reliability operating points for enterprise agent deployment; GDPval names oversight as the excluded residual; Oversight Has a Capacity models escalation spend. What remains open is oversight cost *as a fault-containment mechanism in a propagating-failure scenario*.

## 2. Assigned deep checks

### 2.1 DamageFlow BRS "recovery cost" — how deep?
Verified via wave-1's saved OpenReview record (`raw/or_blast.json`, forum MYvsxnxbCf, ICML 2026 AIWILD, camera-ready revision invited): **BRS decomposes unauthorized tool-effect damage by resource sensitivity × action impact × propagation × recovery cost**, scored by deterministic state-diff oracles on *committed* effects. Critical distinction: BRS's "recovery cost" is a component of the **damage score** (how expensive would it be to undo the harm), **not the cost of the containment mechanism**, and there is no mechanism-comparison-on-cost — BlastGuard is compared to no-defense with 87.5% benign-utility retention. Adversarial framing, single-principal tool use + relay attacks, 80-scenario pilot. **Depth verdict: shallow for CAND-4's purposes — it prices damage, not containment.** (Full text paywalled behind OpenReview bot-check; assessment from the complete abstract + wave-1 records.)

### 2.2 Containing the Cascade (ICERA 2026, DOI 10.1109/ICERA72709.2026.11666599) — cost metrics?
Abstract re-verified via Semantic Scholar record (paperId fbfed6b5…): metrics = **containment rate, cascade depth, blast radius, MTTD + task success**. The circuit-breaker-vs-checkpoint-rollback ablation separates fail-safe from fail-operational **on effectiveness only — zero cost accounting** (no tokens, $, wall-clock, or human time). Simulation-only. **Does not occupy CAND-4's cost axis at all** — but occupies the mechanism-ablation design pattern CAND-4 would use.

### 2.3 AIOpsLab / ITBench / cost-of-pass / Efficient Agents
All four re-verified at abstract level this sweep (B16 had done full-text): AIOpsLab = incident-lifecycle framework (episode token cost per B16 full-text; no mechanism cost comparison, no human time). ITBench = 94 SRE/CISO/CISO scenarios, MTTD/MTTR in seconds; no token/$ cost of mitigation, no human time. Cost-of-Pass = expected $ per correct solution, explicitly steady-state (self-refinement gains "rarely justify their cost" — the closest existing statement that recovery-flavored compute has measurable, sometimes-negative ROI). Efficient Agents = cost-of-pass applied to agent frameworks on GAIA; no faults. **None kills CAND-4; all four are must-cite differentiation targets.**

### 2.4 Human oversight cost in agent supervision
- **Oversight Has a Capacity (2606.08919)** — abstract re-verified: escalation policy spends finite human attention; inverted-U safety vs escalation rate; fatigue + flooding attack. **Prevention/gating of single-agent irreversible actions — not post-fault recovery, no MAS, no propagation.** Occupies "escalation as costed resource."
- **GDPval** — re-verified on openai.com: "frontier models … roughly 100× faster and 100× cheaper than industry experts. However, these figures reflect pure model inference time and API billing rates, and therefore **do not capture the human oversight, iteration, and integration steps** required in real workplace settings." The oversight residual is explicitly named and unmeasured — a quotable gap statement for CAND-4.
- **READY (2609.02095)** — the new occupant: human-oversight burden + cost + reliability as a deployment operating point (§1 #4). Closest existing "priced human oversight" work; must be cited.
- Also from wave 1: AI Control (2312.06942, ICML 2024) — human labor budgeted against automated checking on a safety-usefulness Pareto (adversarial, single-agent coding); FALCON/DeCCaF (capacity/fatigue-constrained learning-to-defer); Nonuniformity Principle (2607.16530, oversight checkpoint placement under human time constraints).

### 2.5 SRE incident-cost methodology transferred to agents
**Occupied.** MTTR-A (2511.20663) is the explicit transfer: "adapts classical dependability theory to agentic orchestration" — MTTR/MTBF/NRR for LLM MAS on LangGraph. ITBench operationalizes MTTD/MTTR for SRE agents. AIOpsLab prices episodes in tokens. Classical recovery-cost theory (Young 1974 checkpoint intervals, Daly 2006, microreboot OSDI 2004, ROC 2002, plan-repair AAMAS 2005) remains importable framing per B16. Fresh searches for "downtime cost"/"incident cost" + LLM agent returned nothing beyond these.

## 4. What survives — precise statement

CAND-4 survives iff it claims exactly this and nothing broader:

> **The first benchmark that (i) injects faults into multi-agent organizational workflows (role/authority/memory/accountability/human boundaries), (ii) lets faults propagate, and (iii) compares heterogeneous containment/recovery mechanisms — retry, verifier, quarantine, rollback, revocation, reroute, AND human escalation — on a common cost-utility Pareto whose cost vector includes tokens, tool calls, wall-clock, and human oversight minutes, with task utility measured under propagation.**

Every broader claim is dead:
- "first to measure recovery cost" → dead (ToolMaze RC, EAS ΔC_rec, Keel, FTDI, AgentTether, AIOpsLab, ITBench, MTTR-A).
- "first cost-utility comparison of recovery mechanisms" → dead single-agent (Keel frontier; Self-Healing Orchestrators budget sweep; CodeRescue) and partially occupied in MAS code-gen (FTDI).
- "cost-of-pass extended to faults" → survives as *framing* (2504.13359 has no fault dimension) but must cite ToolMaze/Keel/FTDI as prior recovery-cost metrics.
- "human oversight cost unmeasured" → dead as stated (READY, Oversight-Has-a-Capacity, AI Control); survives only as "human escalation **as a fault-containment mechanism for propagating organizational faults**, priced against automated alternatives" (confirmed unoccupied).
- "SRE recovery metrics for agents" → dead (MTTR-A, ITBench).

**Mandatory citations/differentiation for CAND-4:** Keel (zenodo.22754511), ToolMaze (2606.05806), MTTR-A (2511.20663), READY (2609.02095), FTDI (10.2139/ssrn.6387100), Self-Healing Orchestrators (2606.01416), CodeRescue (2607.19338, withdrawn), EAS resilience (2608.23839), AgentTether (2607.06273), memory rollback repair (2608.10502), DamageFlow (MYvsxnxbCf), Containing the Cascade (10.1109/ICERA72709.2026.11666599), AIOpsLab (2501.06706), ITBench (2502.05352), cost-of-pass (2504.13359), Efficient Agents (2508.02694), Oversight Has a Capacity (2606.08919), AI Control (2312.06942), GDPval.

## 5. Risk notes for the orchestrator
- **Keel is the most dangerous moving part**: self-published 2026-09-14, manuscript "under review" — if it lands at a venue before MASO review, reviewers may treat the economics-of-recovery framing as occupied even for MAS. CAND-4 must differentiate on multi-agent propagation + organizational boundaries + human escalation, which Keel entirely lacks.
- The single-agent recovery-cost cluster (Keel, ToolMaze, AgentTether, CodeRescue, memory-rollback) is growing fast — expect "just extend Keel to MAS" reviews. The propagation/blast-radius interaction with mechanism cost (cheap containment → larger spread → higher downstream cost) is the phenomenon none of these can express; that is CAND-4's defensible core.
- FTDI shows the MAS code-gen community is already doing budget-aware recovery; a MASO submission needs organizational (non-code-gen) workflows to stay distinct.
