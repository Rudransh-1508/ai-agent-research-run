# ASSASSIN E4 — Kill-the-seed via non-LLM prior art: chaos engineering, microservice fault propagation, distributed fault containment/recovery

**Date:** 2026-09-21
**Seed under attack:** "Organizational failure propagation and recovery in LLM multi-agent systems" — what determines the *organizational blast radius* of a local fault (spread across role, communication, authority/delegation, tool, memory, accountability, human-agent boundaries), and which containment/recovery mechanisms minimize systemic impact while preserving task utility.
**Mission:** Adversarial evidence-gathering. Test whether mature distributed-systems / chaos-engineering prior art kills the seed, and whether the transfer to LLM MAS is publishable or trivial relabeling.

---

## 1. Bottom line

**The non-LLM prior art does NOT kill the seed, but it strips most of its vocabulary of novelty.** "Blast radius," containment, fault injection, circuit breakers, bulkheads, checkpoint/rollback, supervision, and recovery-under-budget are all mature, industrially operationalized concepts (Netflix 2011–2017, Gremlin, AWS FIS, AWS fault-isolation whitepapers, Erlang/OTP, recovery-oriented computing, LDFI). A paper that merely renames "service" → "agent" and re-runs Chaos Monkey would be trivial relabeling and reviewers would say so.

**The bigger threat is not the classical literature — it is a fast-forming 2025–2026 LLM-MAS literature that has already executed slices of the seed.** Most dangerously, *Containing the Cascade* (ICERA 2026) already uses the exact term "blast radius" as a containment metric for failure propagation in tool-using LLM MAS, with a fault-injection benchmark (Cascade-Bench) and a mediator (Sentinel) combining message contracts, a semantic anomaly gate, a circuit breaker, and checkpoint recovery — including a fail-safe vs fail-operational ablation that is precisely the seed's "containment vs task utility" trade-off. AgentChaos (ASE 2026) already does runtime chaos-engineering fault injection into agent systems at the LLM-API layer. The seed survives only in a differentiated form (see §6).

## 5. Does the non-LLM prior art kill the seed?

**No — but it defines the novelty bar.** The seed's *mechanisms* (fault injection, blast-radius scoping, circuit breakers, bulkheads, checkpoint/rollback, supervision, sagas, replication, failure detectors) are all solved and named in distributed systems (§3.1–3.2), and its *organizational* framing has pre-LLM MAS roots (§3.3). A MASO reviewer with any distsys background will ask: "What does this paper teach me that Basiri 2016 + Nygard + Armstrong + Candea do not, beyond renaming services as agents?" If the answer is nothing, it is trivial relabeling and should be rejected even at a workshop.

**However, the transfer is not trivial if it demonstrates where the classical theory breaks.** There are real, articulable mismatches (§6). And the classical literature itself supplies the *methodological template* (chaos experiment discipline, LDFI-style redundancy analysis, ROC metrics) that a good MASO paper can import with citation credit rather than disguising as novelty.

## 6. What would make the transfer non-trivial (the defensible residue)

1. **Semantic faults violate crash/Byzantine assumptions.** Classical fault models assume faults are detectable (crash, omission, timing) or arbitrary-but-replicated-around (Byzantine). LLM agent faults are *plausible-but-wrong content* that passes all syntactic checks and propagates as valid-looking context. Failure detectors (D3) have no semantic oracle; detection itself is an LLM judgment with measurable false-positive/negative rates (L13 shows even attribution is near-unsolved). A paper that formalizes "semantic fault propagation" and shows where Chandra–Toueg-style detectors fail is non-trivial.
2. **Correlated failures break redundancy.** N-version programming, replication (D4, M3), and control/experiment analysis (C5) assume fault independence. LLM agents sharing a foundation model fail *correlatedly* (L10 shows shared architectural priors dominate explicit prompts as contagion drivers). Quantifying correlation-driven blast radius is new.
3. **State = context; recovery = context repair.** Chandy–Lamport snapshots and microreboots (D1, D7) assume restorable process state. Agent state is a natural-language context window plus external side effects; rollback must repair *meaning* (poisoned memory, polluted summaries), not just bytes. Cordon (L8) starts this for tool effects; memory/conversation repair is open.
4. **Propagation medium is interpretable text.** Messages are both data and instruction (prompt infection, L15); there is no checksum. The "channel" can be semantically compromised without any transport fault — no distsys analogue.
5. **Organizational boundaries are the genuinely unoccupied layer.** Role, authority/delegation, accountability, and human-agent handoffs have no clean classical analogue (capability systems and confused-deputy are the closest). L9 covers adversarial privilege-boundary containment at small scale; the *accidental-fault, organization-wide* version — and its interaction with the occupied delegation-invariants proposal (maso/policy_preserving_delegation_research_proposal.md) — is open. This is also the most MASO-native angle.
6. **Utility-preserving containment trade-off under semantic degradation.** "Minimize blast radius while preserving task utility" maps to fail-safe vs fail-operational (L1's ablation) and graceful degradation (D10), but utility for LLM MAS is fuzzy task quality, not availability/latency SLOs. Defining steady state and utility metrics for chaos experiments on semantic systems (C3 assumes measurable steady state) is a real methodological gap.

## 7. Is the transfer publishable at MASO 2026? Honest answer

- **Naive form ("apply chaos engineering to LLM MAS"): NO.** Occupied by L4 (framing), L2 (executed method, ASE 2026), L5 (single-agent benchmark), and above all **L1, which already uses "blast radius" + containment + circuit breaker + checkpoint recovery + utility trade-off for tool-using LLM MAS**. Reviewers will call it relabeling.
- **Differentiated form: YES, plausibly strong for a workshop.** The defensible version is: *real-execution* (not simulation, contra L1) measurement of organizational blast radius across boundaries L1 does not touch (authority/delegation, memory, accountability, human-agent), with containment mechanisms evaluated against the utility-preservation trade-off, explicitly framed as "where distributed-systems fault containment theory transfers and where it breaks" (§6 items 1–4 as findings, not assumptions). L1's weaknesses (simulated message passing, minor venue, no organizational boundaries, no real models) are exactly the headroom.
- **Timing risk is severe.** L1 (Jul 2026), L2/L3 (Aug 2026), L9/L11 (Sep 2026) all landed within the last ~10 weeks. This space is being colonized in real time; a MASO submission must cite and differentiate from all of them, and another assassin should re-check for October 2026 drops before submission.

## 8. Explicit answer: does anything here kill or subsume the seed hypothesis?

- **Killed outright:** "chaos engineering for LLM MAS" as a framing (L4, L2); LLM-API-layer fault injection for agents (L2, ASE 2026); single-agent chaos-style reliability benchmarking (L5).
- **Substantially subsumed:** the seed's core loop — blast-radius/containment metrics + fault-injection benchmark + circuit-breaker/checkpoint containment + fail-safe-vs-fail-operational utility trade-off for tool-using LLM MAS — by **L1 (Containing the Cascade, ICERA 2026, DOI 10.1109/ICERA72709.2026.11666599)**. Any MASO submission that does not cite and clearly out-scope this paper is dead on arrival.
- **Not subsumed (the surviving seed):** (a) real LLM-MAS execution rather than calibrated simulation; (b) organizational boundaries beyond message passing — authority/delegation (adversarial-only in L9), shared/corrupted memory, accountability propagation, human-agent escalation; (c) the theory question of *which* classical containment results fail under semantic, correlated, interpretable-medium faults (§6). A paper delivering (a)+(b) with (c) as analysis is defensible at a workshop; anything less is relabeling of §3 plus L1.
