## VERDICT CHANGE: STAKES UNCLEAR → STAKES PRESENT (deferred-accountability reading)

The earlier session looked for the mechanism in observability backends and
agent frameworks and correctly found that they keep per-agent records and
respond to budget pressure by dropping whole episodes. That conclusion stands
for those systems. It was the wrong place to look. Two things were missed, and
both are load-bearing:

1. **The binding budget in agent systems is the context window, not the trace
   store** — and unlike trace retention, it is resolved by *within-episode
   aggregation*, exactly the compression geometry the earlier session searched
   for and did not find.
2. **A deployed multi-agent settlement system has already suffered C39's
   failure mode and paid to fix it** — Bittensor's weight-copying problem.

Neither finding rescues the strong form of C39's claim (a *storage*-bounded
record that must pay real money). That specific conjunction is still not
evidenced, and §4 says so. But C39 no longer rests on a hypothetical.

## E1 — Bittensor: the mechanism fired in a deployed system, and the fix was a record change

**What the system is.** Each Bittensor subnet has validators who score miners.
*"In each Bittensor subnet, each validator scores — or 'weights' — each miner,
producing what is referred to as a weight vector. The weight vectors for each
validator in a subnet are combined into a weight matrix. This matrix determines
emissions to miners in the subnet based on the consensus evaluation of their
performance, according to Yuma Consensus."*

**The C39 mapping, stated exactly:**

| C39 object | Bittensor object |
|---|---|
| agents whose effort is at stake | validators |
| effort | actually evaluating miners, vs. copying |
| the retained record | submitted weight vectors + Yuma consensus over them |
| deferred settlement from the record alone | dividends, paid per tempo from that matrix |
| reconstruction use of the same record | recovering which miners are good (emissions) |
| unilateral effort sensitivity | does a validator's own evaluation work change its pay? |

**The failure.** Settlement rewards *agreement with the merged record*: VTrust is
*"a ranking based on how well the validator's weights match the consensus"*, and
*"to score well in Vtrust, your weights must be similar to those of every other
validator."* So a validator that performs no evaluation and submits the
stake-weighted average of everyone else's vector is, in the record,
indistinguishable from — in fact better aligned than — one that did the work.
Reported outcome: *"Optimized weight copiers achieve higher validator dividends
per stake than honest validators, making weight copying more profitable than
honest work."* That is unilateral effort sensitivity at or below zero, in
production, at scale.

**The fix is a record-design intervention, not a monitoring one.** Commit-Reveal
encrypts submitted weights with Drand time-lock so they are invisible for a set
number of tempos; copiers are forced onto stale data and fall away from
consensus. Simulated on historical subnet data, copier dividend-per-stake falls
with the reveal delay and *"results converged when the commit reveal weights
interval reached 1800 (5 tempos), with a longer interval yielding little benefit
but slowing down the evaluation of miners."*

That last clause is the C39 frontier in the wild: **lengthening the delay buys
incentive margin and costs evaluation quality, and the operator picks a point on
the curve.** It is a different axis from record *size*, and the paper must say
so (see §4), but it is the same trade between what the record supports for
reconstruction and what it supports for payment.

**Independent corroboration:** the problem has a peer-reviewed footprint —
*"Poster: Solving the Free-rider Problem in Bittensor"*, ACM CCS 2024.

Sources: [taostats — Weight Copying](https://docs.taostats.io/docs/weight-copying),
[Bittensor docs — Weight Copying](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/weight-copying-in-bittensor),
[Bittensor docs — Commit Reveal](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/concepts/commit-reveal),
[Opentensor working paper, 29 May 2024](https://docs.learnbittensor.org/papers/BT_Weight_Copier-29May2024.pdf),
[CCS 2024 poster](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3658644.3691414),
[taostats glossary](https://docs.taostats.io/docs/glossary).

**Caveat, recorded up front.** Bittensor's record bound is cryptoeconomic and
temporal (what the chain accepts, and when it becomes visible), not a storage
bound. It evidences that the *consequence* — a merged record destroying
unilateral effort sensitivity, with real money attached — is real and expensive.
It does not by itself evidence the *size* mechanism. E2 does.

## E2 — The binding budget is the context window, and it is resolved by aggregating within the episode

This is the item `real_world_relevance.md` listed as missing ("any production
system that compresses a multi-agent trace by aggregating across agents rather
than by dropping episodes. Nothing found does this"). It is the default
architecture of 2026 multi-agent systems:

* *"A subagent's messy tool output stays inside the subagent, and only the
  summary reaches the lead, so the orchestrator's window stays clean."*
* *"More bluntly, only the subagent's final message returns to the parent."*
* *"Within a session, the orchestrator's conversation grows until it nears the
  model's context-window limit, at which point the system compacts it,
  summarizing the older exchanges to free space. On each compaction the
  orchestrator is handed back a summary of its still-running sub-agents, any
  background computations, and its current task list."*
* Acknowledged as lossy in a way that reaches governance: *"compaction can
  silently erase safety constraints as governance decays."*

Why this matters more than the trace-store argument:

1. **The budget is hard and physical.** A context window cannot be bought around
   the way retention days can. Sampling is not available as a response — you
   cannot drop 99.9% of *this* episode and still run it.
2. **The response is exactly the geometry C39 models.** A k-agent fan-out
   collapses to one summary paragraph in the parent's record. That is
   aggregation across agents, within one episode, under a hard bound.
3. **The compressed object is then the sole input to what happens next** — the
   orchestrator's re-planning, its decision to re-run or trust a subagent, and
   any post-hoc report. Whatever the parent later concludes about what a
   subagent did, it concludes from the summary.

Sources: [Redis — sub-agents, splitting context](https://redis.io/blog/sub-agents-splitting-context-specialized-ai-agents/),
[Wire — sub-agent context isolation](https://usewire.io/blog/sub-agent-context-isolation-fixes-context-rot/),
[Claude Code subagents and orchestration guide](https://hidekazu-konishi.com/entry/claude_code_subagents_and_orchestration_guide.html),
[Vercel — multi-agent systems architecture](https://vercel.com/i/multi-agent-systems).

**Quantitative bridge to the theory.** §4 of `research_state.md` gives the free
allowance as `log2(n/2)` bits out of `n`: at `n = 20` a record is flat only if it
retains 16.5–17.0 of 20 bits of per-agent resolution. A natural-language summary
of a 20-subagent fan-out is nowhere near 17 bits of per-agent resolution. So the
standard architecture sits **well inside the tension regime** — the reading
`research_state.md` §4 already flagged as available ("On that reading real
records sit inside the tension regime") but could not previously attach to a real
compression step. It now can.

## E3 — The regime is an active research problem, and the incentive question inside it is open

**Nian, Cao, Zhu, Zou, Luan, Zhang, Zhao — "When Only the Final Text Survives:
Implicit Execution Tracing for Multi-Agent Auditing", arXiv:2603.17445v5,
1 Aug 2026** (USC, Toronto, UIC). Verbatim from the abstract:

> *"When a multi-agent system produces an incorrect or harmful answer, who is
> accountable if execution logs and agent identifiers are unavailable? In
> practice, generated content is often detached from its execution environment
> due to privacy or system boundaries, leaving the final text as the only
> auditable artifact. Existing attribution methods rely on full execution traces
> and thus become ineffective in such metadata-deprived settings."*

Two things follow.

1. **C39's setting is somebody else's stated problem**, published, at a strong
   set of institutions, six weeks ago. The "does this regime exist" objection is
   now answerable by citation.
2. **The niche is intact.** IET assumes *"agents are assumed honest-but-
   instrumented"* and its target is provenance recovery, not strategic effort.
   There is no incentive constraint, no payment, no bound: their question is "can
   I attribute this text", C39's is "what must the record keep so that paying
   from it still rewards effort". They are complementary, and IET is the natural
   citation for the setting and the natural contrast for the contribution.

Adjacent 2026 work, same shape, same gap — compression policies optimised for
task performance with no incentive term:
[TRACE, arXiv:2606.00611](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.00611),
[TRACER, arXiv:2608.29363](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29363),
[VICT, arXiv:2608.28128](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28128),
[RL through orchestration traces, arXiv:2605.02801](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02801v1).

## E4 — Deployed multi-agent payment infrastructure now exists (weaker evidence, reported anyway)

The earlier session found "platform fee splits and proposals". That is now out of
date:

* **Virtuals Agent Commerce Protocol** (public beta from 3 July): a four-phase
  model — *"Request, Negotiation, Transaction, and Evaluation — coordinated by
  three roles: Client, Provider, and Evaluator"*, where *"payments and
  deliverables are held in escrow until an Evaluator verifies the work against a
  cryptographically signed Proof of Agreement"*, and *"evaluator agents
  specialize in verifying outputs and receive a share of the transaction value."*
* **Olas / Autonolas**: Proof-of-Active-Agent pays from on-chain KPI counters
  accumulated per epoch.

**How to use this and how not to.** These establish that deferred, record-based
settlement between autonomous agents is now deployed rather than proposed —
which is what the earlier session could not show. They do **not** establish a
bounded record: ACP settles per job against a signed agreement, which is
per-provider and therefore SAFE in P4's classification. Report them as "the
setting is now real", never as "the tension occurs here".

Sources: [RockawayX on ACP](https://www.rockawayx.com/insights/virtuals-agent-commerce-protocol-in-public-beta),
[Virtuals whitepaper — commerce layer](https://whitepaper.virtuals.io/about-virtuals/commerce-layer),
[Delphi — ACP](https://members.delphidigital.io/reports/virtuals-acp-open-coordination-for-digital-labor),
[Olas staking](https://olas.network/staking).

## 4. What is still NOT established

3. Whether any operator has *knowingly* traded attribution against record size.
   Bittensor's 1800-block tuning is the closest thing found, and it is a timing
   trade, not a size trade.

## 5. Disposal of the unobtainable Arulanand et al. (2023)

It is cited, not relied on. Concretely:

* Cite it as existing work on `gamma_2` for special hypercube classes, from its
  public abstract only, and say nothing about its contents.
* State plainly in the paper that our values are computed independently and ship
  with machine-checkable certificates (explicit codes, plus MILP infeasibility
  proofs below each value). That is what a referee can check; the provenance of a
  coincident published value does not weaken a certified computation.
* The exposure is bounded: only the *values* could collide, never the reduction,
  and the values are a table, not the contribution.
* Two mitigations found this session that partly substitute for it:
  * `gamma_2(Q_n)` for n = 2,3,4,6,7 coincides with the well-established
    `gamma(Q_{n+1})` (published table for `gamma(Q_n)`, n = 1..10:
    1, 2, 2, 4, 7, 12, 16, 32, 62, 107–120) — and **breaks at n = 5**
    (`gamma_2(Q_5) = 10` vs `gamma(Q_6) = 12`). The coincidence is therefore not
    a theorem, but it does independently corroborate our 16 and 32, which are the
    values a referee is most likely to spot-check.
  * `gamma_2(Q_5) = 10` needs no external authority at all: Fink–Jacobson gives
    `>= ceil(64/7) = 10`, and we exhibit a 10-element 2-dominating set. That
    value is closed by a two-line certificate.

## 6. New prior-art direction found (belongs in P1, flagged here)

`gamma_2(Q_n)` sits next to **multiple coverings of radius 1** in coding theory —
a `mu`-fold `r`-covering is a code in which *"every binary word of length n is
within Hamming distance r of at least mu codewords"*. A 2-fold 1-covering is
exactly 2-domination **plus** the extra requirement that codewords themselves be
covered twice (each centre needs a centre neighbour). Our n = 9 algebraic code
has **zero internal edges**, so it is 2-dominating but not a 2-fold 1-covering —
the two invariants genuinely differ, and the difference is precisely the
structure that blocked shrink-only local search in session 2.

Consequences:

* The literature to cite alongside Fink–Jacobson is Cohen–Honkala–Litsyn–Lobstein,
  *Covering Codes*, plus the multifold-covering line
  ([multifold packings in Hamming graphs, arXiv:1902.00023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.00023)).
* **Lead for closing `K_flat(9)`:** published bounds on `mu`-fold 1-coverings of
  length 9 would bracket `gamma_2(Q_9)` from above for free. Not attempted here.

## 7. Consequence for C39

The engineering case that `research_state.md` §4 said "turns entirely on whether
real records keep per-agent resolution or aggregate it" now has a two-part
answer: **trace stores keep it; context windows do not.** The compression that
matters happens inside the episode, under a budget nobody can buy their way out
of, and the artifact it produces is the one the next decision reads.

P4 is no longer C39's main exposure. The main exposure is now the gap in §4: the
paper must claim the tension for *deferred accountability*, and be explicit that
the money-plus-storage-bound conjunction is not yet observed in one system.
