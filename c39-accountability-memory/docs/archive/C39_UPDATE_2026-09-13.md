## 1. T1 has a short proof, and the current proof has a gap

### 1.1 The decomposition

After normalizing each agent's bounded payment to endpoints and flipping input
bits to align directions, the incentive condition is exactly: every hypercube
edge in direction `i` raises `q` by at least `gamma`. Write

    q(s) = gamma*|s| + r(s)

Then the constraint is precisely that `r` is monotone on the cube. So the
feasible encoder set is the Minkowski sum of `gamma * (random-bit sampler)` and
the monotone cone. With the antipodal decoder the objective is linear:

    D = E[|S|] + E[q(S)(n - 2|S|)]
      = n/2 + gamma*E[|S|(n-2|S|)] + E[r(S)(n-2|S|)]
      = n/2 - gamma*n/2 + E[r(S)(n-2|S|)]

using `E[|S|(n-2|S|)] = -n/2` for uniform `S`. Minimizing a linear functional
over the monotone cone with range `[0, 1-n*gamma]` puts `r` at
`(1-n*gamma)*1{|S| > n/2}`, i.e. majority. With `E[(2|S|-n)^+] = n/2 - M_n`:

    D = M_n + n*gamma*((n-1)/2 - M_n)

This is T1. It also explains why the frontier is LINEAR in gamma rather than
curved: the constraint set is an affine shift of a cone and the objective is
linear. Present T1 this way. It is shorter, it makes the structure visible, and
it is what a referee will reconstruct anyway.

### 1.2 Proof gap in the current write-up

> **RESOLVED 2026-09-15.** The corrected order is used in the paper, and the
> final step (minimising over decoder pairs for general odd n) is proved in
> `paper/sections/A-proofs.tex`, Proposition 14 — by a Rademacher coupling
> that needs no averaging step at all. See `experiment_log.md` E30 and
> `verify_all.py` block E.

`D(q)` under optimal decoding is a min of linear functionals, hence CONCAVE in
`q`. Permutation-averaging therefore INCREASES distortion: `D(q̄) >= D(q)`. The
symmetrization step as currently described runs the wrong way for a lower
bound. The correct order is: fix the decoder pair, average (objective now
linear), optimize over `q`, then optimize over decoder pairs. Fix the write-up.

### 1.3 Uniformity is load-bearing

The input-flip step that aligns per-agent payment orientations is only valid
because the uniform prior is flip-invariant. Section 17's nonuniform tests will
break the proof technique, not merely shift a constant. Expect to need a new
argument, not a perturbation of this one.

## 2. NEW: payment-decoder elimination theorem

**Theorem.** Fix a stochastic encoder `q` with `q[s,y] = P(Y=y | S=s)`. Agent
`i` admits a bounded payment decoder `p_i` in `[0,1]^K` achieving all-profile
unilateral margin `gamma` if and only if

    min over lambda in simplex(teammate profiles) of  (1/2)*|| E_lambda[d_i] ||_1  >=  gamma

where `d_i(s_-i)[y] = q[(1,s_-i), y] - q[(0,s_-i), y]`.

**Proof.** Feasibility means `max_{p in [0,1]^K} min_{s_-i} <p, d_i(s_-i)> >= gamma`.
The inner min over a finite set equals the min over its convex hull. The
objective is bilinear, the cube is convex and compact, so minimax applies:

    max_p min_lambda <p, E_lambda[d]> = min_lambda max_p <p, E_lambda[d]>
                                      = min_lambda sum_y (E_lambda[d])_y^+

Since `sum_y d_y = 0` for every profile, `sum_y (E_lambda[d])_y^+ = (1/2)||E_lambda[d]||_1`. ∎

### Consequences

1. `p` is eliminated. The problem becomes an optimization over `q` alone. The
   bilinear MILP that timed out at n=7 is no longer the right computation.
2. Taking `lambda` a point mass gives the clean necessary condition: **every
   hypercube edge must move the retained label distribution by at least gamma
   in total variation.** This is the natural general-K statement of the
   constraint and the right way to state the phenomenon in the paper.
3. At `K=2` it collapses to T1's signed edge condition.
4. T2 becomes one line: if a codeword and all its radius-one neighbours map to
   a single label, every edge TV is zero, hence `gamma = 0`. The current derived
   bound `D - D0 >= min(2^-n, 3*gamma/(n+1))` is weak because it was obtained
   the hard way; redo the near-perfect-rate regime through the TV condition.

## 3. Computational results

### 3.1 T1 reproduction (exact, `exp1b.py`)

Exhaustive enumeration of decoder pairs reduced to `S_n` orbits (representatives
indexed by counts `(n00,n01,n10,n11)`, i.e. `C(n+3,3)` instead of `4^n`),
cross-checked against full enumeration at n=3 and n=5. LP over `q` for each pair.

| n | gamma range | max deviation from formula |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 0 .. 1/3 | 0 |
| 5 | 0 .. 1/5 | 0 |
| 7 | 0 .. 1/7 | 4.4e-16 |
| 9 | 0 .. 1/9 | 2.7e-15 |

n=9 extends the arbitrary-payment one-bit result past the recorded range.
In every case the optimizer landed exactly on the conjectured encoder
`q(s) = gamma*|s| + (1-n*gamma)*1{|s| > n/2}`. Orientation invariance across
all 8 sign patterns at n=3: spread 1.1e-16.

### 3.2 Certified general-K brackets at n=3 (`exp3_bracket.py`)

- Lower bound: point-mass TV relaxation as a MILP. Valid because it is implied
  by the elimination theorem.
- Upper bound: payment decoders restricted to vertices `p_i in {0,1}^K`, with
  products represented exactly by McCormick inequalities (exact for binary `p`).
  Every solution is genuinely feasible, so the value is a valid upper bound.
- Distortion uses coordinate-wise MAP reconstruction, optimal for Hamming loss:
  `D = sum_{i,y} min( P(S_i=0,Y=y), P(S_i=1,Y=y) )`, encoded with binaries.

| K | D0 | D at gamma=1/3 | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.750 | 1.000 | certified, matches T1 |
| 3 | 0.625 | LB 0.708, UB 0.750 | bracket OPEN |
| 4 | 0.500 | 0.500 | certified FLAT |
| 8 | 0 | 0 | certified FLAT |

All certified rows had MIP gap 0.0.

### 3.3 The flatness result

At n=3, K=4 the frontier is completely flat: `D = D0 = 0.5` for every gamma
tested up to 0.6, certified on both sides. The incentive constraint costs
nothing. Binary-payment feasibility only breaks at gamma = 0.75, and there the
lower bound is rising rather than a low-margin tradeoff appearing.

So at n=3 the reconstruction-incentive tension exists at K=2 and K=3 and
vanishes at K=4. The phenomenon lives in the regime `K < 2^(n-1)`, not in
compressed records generally.

### 3.4 n=5, partial

- K=2: tradeoff confirmed exactly, excess 0.328125 at gamma=0.15, MIP gap 0.
- K=3: UB 1.5625 against D0 1.375, but with MIP gap 0.41. This does NOT prove a
  tradeoff, it only fails to rule one out. Record as inconclusive.
- K>=4: did not finish.

## 4. Corrected priority ordering

Section 26 makes prior art the next task. That is now second. The flatness
result attacks SIGNIFICANCE, and significance is where C39 is currently weaker
than novelty.

### P0 — Flatness threshold scaling  [BLOCKING]

Determine `K_flat(n)`, the smallest number of labels at which the frontier
becomes flat.

- If `K_flat(n)` grows like `2^(n-1)`: the tension survives any sub-exponential
  record. Significance claim holds. Proceed.
- If `K_flat(n)` grows like `n`, `n+1`, or `2n`: any record holding a couple of
  bits per agent escapes the tension, and the organizational-memory framing is a
  claim about a regime no real system operates in. **Downgrade significance and
  consider reframing or killing.**

n=3 gives `K_flat = 4`, which is consistent with `2^(n-1)`, `n+1`, and `2n`
simultaneously. n=5 separates them: `16` vs `6` vs `10`. **This is the single
most informative number in the program right now.**

Cheap decisive test: only the UPPER bound is needed. `UB(K,gamma) == D0(K)`
proves flatness, because UB is attained by a genuinely feasible construction.
UB has only `2nK` binaries and scales far better than the LB model.

Predeclared kill test: if `K_flat(5) <= 10`, the significance claim in its
current form is dead.

### P1 — Prior art, reframed

The section 15 search list points at mechanism design and strategic source
coding. Once T1 is stated as "minimize expected Hamming reconstruction error of
a uniform n-bit source from a K-ary summary subject to all discrete derivatives
being at least gamma", the nearest neighbours are **analysis of Boolean
functions** and **one-bit distributed estimation**, not contracting. One-bit
summaries of uniform bits under Hamming loss with majority optimal is close to
folklore in the CEO-problem literature. Influence-constrained extremal problems
on the hypercube are well worked.

Search these FIRST, because a collision there kills faster and cheaper:

- monotone-cone linear programs on the hypercube
- influence-constrained extremal problems, minimum-influence conditions
- one-bit / finite-rate distributed estimation, CEO problem
- rate-distortion with a Lipschitz-from-below constraint

Keep the section 15 economics list, but expect those to yield a related-work
paragraph rather than a kill, because the assumption mismatch is large.

Predeclared kill test: any theorem giving `D*(2,gamma)` under equivalent
assumptions kills the T1 novelty claim outright. Do not rescue with terminology.
