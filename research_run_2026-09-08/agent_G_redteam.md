# Agent G — adversarial novelty and publishability red-team

**Cutoff:** 8 September 2026.  
**Inputs read:** `agent_A_cartographer.md`, `agent_B_gap_hunter.md`, `agent_C_novelty_initial.md`, `agent_E_feasibility.md`, and `agent_H_opportunity.md`. I also used the independent reviewer report only as a cross-check.  
**Interpretation:** “Retain” below means *plausible enough for a second, candidate-specific audit*. It does not mean verified novelty. No candidate supports a safe “first” claim.

## Executive verdict

The initial reports contain many good questions, but most are publishability traps: they join two mature literatures, add one deployment complication to a 2025–2026 method, or measure a failure without forcing a new scientific explanation. Two especially dangerous papers surfaced in this pass:

- [Verification Required: The Impact of Information Credibility on AI Persuasion / MixTalk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00970) already gives a strategic sender, a receiver with a limited costly-verification budget, and a learned receiver policy. It kills B03 in its advertised form.
- [MemTX: Transactional Belief Commit for Stateful Agent Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23929) already attaches evidence/provenance/validity to shared records and performs typed cascading repair after retraction, with a cascade-completeness invariant. It kills B15 almost exactly.

After consolidation, I retain **seven** directions, all conditionally. Four are medium-confidence plausible gaps; three are low-to-medium-confidence fallbacks. Everything else should be rejected or treated only as an ablation inside one of those seven.

## Verdict rubric

- **Reject—occupied:** a close paper already studies the central interaction, not merely one ingredient.
- **Reject—incremental combination:** the remaining difference is a parameter, deployment complication, or juxtaposition of established methods.
- **Reject—weak scientific core:** likely benchmark, systems, prompt, or metric paper without a new explanatory object.
- **Retain—conditional:** no exact match found in this pass, but the closest work substantially constrains the claim.

## Final surviving set (maximum eight; seven retained)

### S1. Causal attribution of multi-agent non-stationarity

**Question:** Can an agent distinguish exogenous drift, independently learning peers, and ego-induced peer adaptation when their passive histories are observationally equivalent?  
**Strongest killer:** [Teaching and Leading an Ad Hoc Teammate](https://u.cs.biu.ac.il/~sarit/data/articles/AIJ13-adhoc.pdf).  
**Why still different:** prior work exploits teaching/influence; the proposed paper would make *source identifiability* the object and require controlled interventions to select among qualitatively different responses.  
**Mandatory kill test:** construct paired environments with identical passive trace distributions; if ordinary opponent-model prediction plus a generic exploration bonus matches an intervention-aware method, reject.  
**Status:** plausible gap, medium confidence.

### S2. Stable translation under bilateral online protocol drift

**Question:** Can two independently trained agents track compatible semantics when both protocols update online and there is no common stimulus, paired corpus, or centralized gradient?  
**Strongest killer:** [Verifiable Semantics for Agent-to-Agent Communication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16424).  
**Why still different:** certification assumes testable shared observable events; the residual deliberately removes that grounding and studies coupled tracking stability.  
**Mandatory kill test:** compare frozen-side CLAP, certification/renegotiation, independent online translators, and a no-translation co-adaptation baseline across matched drift rates. If timescale separation alone solves it, reject.  
**Status:** plausible gap, medium-low confidence.

### S3. Strategic evolving capability under coalition-only feedback and no transfers

**Question:** When complementary agents’ capabilities change through assignment and effort, what can an allocator identify or guarantee if it observes only coalition success and agents can misreport?  
**Strongest killer:** [Multi-Agent Contract Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13654).  
**Why still different:** contract work uses outcome-contingent payments and often individual outcomes; the residual removes transfers and individual feedback while making type dynamics endogenous.  
**Mandatory kill test:** first prove non-identifiability or a regret lower bound in a binary-skill model. If the problem becomes identifiable after simple randomized assignments or if a contextual combinatorial-bandit baseline wins, reject.  
**Status:** plausible gap, medium confidence.

### S4. Decentralized policy-realization regret for language agents

**Question:** Given an exact contingent Dec-POMDP joint policy, when and why do black-box language agents fail to realize their local controllers under private histories, and can a compiler/repair procedure close that gap without privileged state?  
**Strongest killer:** [Commitment-Driven Distributed Joint Policy Search](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas07/html/tec16_08.html).  
**Why still different:** old work synthesizes distributed policies/commitments for modeled agents; the residual measures and repairs realization by black-box language agents against an exact executable reference.  
**Mandatory kill test:** compare direct finite-state-controller execution, serialized controller text, token-matched chat, centralized state leakage, and model-size scaling. If syntax/rendering explains most regret or larger models erase it, reject.  
**Status:** plausible gap, medium confidence; likely evaluation/Findings unless the repair algorithm is substantive.

### S5. Reputation inversion from structured blame errors

**Question:** Can empirically measured attribution bias make blame-based sanctions select less competent teams than matched random exclusion, and can uncertainty-aware updating prevent the inversion?  
**Strongest killer:** [Incentive Based Cooperation in Multi-Agent Auctions](https://cdn.aaai.org/ocs/4325/4325-19492-1-PB.pdf).  
**Why still different:** noisy-reputation thresholds are old; the residual is a role- and trace-dependent error kernel estimated from current collaborative agents plus an ordering-inversion theorem.  
**Mandatory kill test:** estimate the kernel on one task/system family, preregister the welfare ordering, and test held out. If symmetric-noise theory explains it or no inversion occurs, reject immediately.  
**Status:** plausible but fragile, low-medium confidence.

### S6. Endogenous invalidation of shared experience by teammate learning

**Question:** Can a team predict when another agent’s policy update invalidates previously useful joint experience, separately from ordinary recency or exogenous environment drift?  
**Strongest killer:** [Useful Memories Become Faulty](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12978).  
**Why still different:** that paper isolates consolidation failure in a single agent; the residual requires one learner’s evolution to change another agent’s memory validity.  
**Mandatory kill test:** frozen peers, co-evolving peers, and matched exogenous-drift controls; no memory, recency, provenance/change-conditioned, and oracle-validity baselines. If recency matches the proposed method, reject.  
**Status:** plausible gap, medium-low confidence.

### S7. Incentive-compatible maintenance of persistent shared memory

**Question:** What mechanism induces costly verification, challenge, supersession, and repair of persistent shared artifacts whose future value is delayed and coalition-dependent?  
**Strongest killer:** [Everyone Contributes!](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02076).  
**Why still different:** MAC-SPGG rewards sequential task contributions, not lifecycle maintenance of persistent, depreciating, reusable evidence. But “memory commons” itself is already an explicit 2026 framing.  
**Mandatory kill test:** solve a minimal repeated game before building an LLM system; compare no pool, cooperative pool, flat reward, access pricing, downstream-use reward, and oracle curation. If payoff changes do not alter open-model behavior or the equilibrium is a standard public-goods transfer, reject.  
**Status:** plausible but fragile, medium-low confidence.

## Strongest attacks on the seven survivors

1. **S1 may be an old dual-control/teaching problem with new labels.** The defense requires a formal observational-equivalence construction and action-selection consequence, not a better classifier.
2. **S2 may be ordinary non-stationary domain adaptation.** Meaning must remain relational and unobservable except through joint outcomes; otherwise the multi-agent premise disappears.
3. **S3 may be impossible by design.** That is acceptable only if the impossibility is sharp and a minimal extra signal restores learnability. An arbitrary heuristic allocator is not.
4. **S4 may be a benchmark for model instruction-following.** Exact controller semantics, private histories, and an algorithmic compiler/repair contribution must be load-bearing.
5. **S5 may reproduce classical asymmetric-label-noise reputation results.** Real structured kernels and out-of-family prediction are non-negotiable.
6. **S6 may collapse to “old data are stale.”** Matched exogenous drift and recency baselines are the decisive controls.
7. **S7 may collapse to MAC-SPGG or a data-market transfer.** Persistence, lifecycle actions, delayed externalities, and equilibrium differences must each change a result.

## Ideas that should not be rescued by adding more components

- B03 after MixTalk: do not add reputation, memory, or extra LLM roles to manufacture distance.
- B15 after MemTX: do not rename transactional repair as decentralized truth maintenance.
- B04 after Epistemic Sybil Resistance: do not make a new leaderboard for provenance-aware voting.
- B11/H11 after identity/Sybil work: cryptographic lineage plumbing is a systems contribution unless paired with a new cooperation guarantee.
- H08 after strategic-disclosure networks: adding RL to an existing information-transmission game is not a new question.
- B12: do not combine chance constraints, Byzantine faults, shielding, and lossy communication in one paper; each extra assumption obscures the theorem.
- A3/B13: do not ship a coordination metric without an estimand, identification conditions, and a result that reverses a substantive conclusion.

## Final calibration to the parent synthesis

- **Verified gap:** none. The searches support missing *formulations in the inspected set*, not exhaustive absence.
- **Plausible gaps requiring deeper candidate-specific verification:** S1–S7.
- **Rejected / already addressed:** every other consolidated family in the ledger.

The most defensible top-three ordering after this attack is **S1 causal source attribution**, **S3 strategic evolving capability with coalition-only feedback**, then **S4 decentralized policy-realization regret**. S5 is the cheapest empirical kill test. S2, S6, and S7 are scientifically interesting but sit closest to active 2026 literatures and therefore need the strongest citation-forward audit.
