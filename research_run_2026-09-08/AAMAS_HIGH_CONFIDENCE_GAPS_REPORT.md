This program did not find a gap that can honestly be called verified or clearly novel after an exhaustive search. These are opportunities for a focused paper, not safe claims of priority.

### Cross-paper contradictions that matter

1. Strong benchmark return does not prove closed-loop coordination: SMAC-style scores can coexist with open-loop behavior.
2. More agents can improve independent sampling while interactive communication reduces diversity; team size is not evidence of multi-agent intelligence.
3. Debate gains can disappear against compute-matched self-consistency unless interaction creates new evidence.
4. Known capability is not compatibility: individually strong agents can pair poorly.
5. Formal Byzantine agreement does not solve strategic semantic ambiguity, but adding natural language does not by itself create novelty.
6. Exact rational models may predict no collusion while bounded learning agents learn it; the learner class must be explicit.
7. Simulation partner models can fail badly with humans; transfer claims require separate human evidence.

## Deep Dive: Research Direction 1

### One-line idea

Build a causal decision theory for recognizing whether observed change comes from the environment, an independently learning peer, or a peer adapting in response to the focal agent.

### The research gap

Multi-agent learning methods generally react to non-stationarity or model a changing opponent. The unresolved residual is **source identification under observational equivalence**: different causal processes can produce the same passive trajectory distribution but demand opposite interventions.

### Why existing methods fail

A change-point detector identifies that behavior changed, not why. Opponent models can predict a peer while conflating environment context with strategic adaptation. Opponent shaping assumes that the peer is responsive; robust control often assumes the opposite. Choosing the wrong response can waste probes, destabilize a cooperative convention, or manipulate a teammate unnecessarily.

### Closest prior work

- [LeMOL: learning opponent learning awareness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03923)
- [MBOM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01843)
- [Influencing Towards Stable Multi-Agent Interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08229)
- [Meta-MAPG](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/kim21g.html)
- [SOM: Structured Opponent Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07301)
- [ShapeLLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08255)
- [Causal-context-aware teammate modeling with risk-constrained planning](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0950705125022786)

### Why this is different

The closest 2026 causal-context work separates contextual confounders from teammate strategy, but does not ask whether a teammate would have changed under a counterfactual focal policy. The proposed paper makes the three-way source distinction and the value/cost of interventions the central estimand.

### Research question

Under what conditions can a decentralized agent distinguish exogenous drift, autonomous peer learning, and ego-caused peer adaptation from local histories and bounded interventions, and use the distinction to select tracking, robust control, or shaping?

### Falsifiable hypothesis

For passively observationally matched process pairs, a source-aware policy using a fixed intervention budget will reduce dynamic regret and harmful shaping relative to prediction-matched change-point, opponent-modeling, and shaping baselines; permuting the inferred source label will remove the advantage.

### Proposed approach

1. Construct process pairs with matched passive histories but different causal response to a controlled ego action.
2. Maintain a posterior over causal source models rather than only peer policy parameters.
3. Select safe diagnostic interventions by expected decision value, not classification accuracy alone.
4. Condition the control mode—track, hedge, or shape—on posterior uncertainty.

### Formalization

Use a factored partially observable Markov game with physical state (x_t), environment regime (e_t), peer parameter (theta_t), and latent source (c):

- exogenous: (e_{t+1}=F_e(e_t,xi_t)), peer fixed;
- independent learner: (theta_{t+1}=F_0(theta_t,h^{-i}_t,eta_t));
- responsive learner: (theta_{t+1}=F_1(theta_t,h^{-i}_t,a^i_t,pi^i_t,eta_t)).

The key theory targets are: (i) passive non-identifiability; (ii) sufficient excitation/intervention conditions; and (iii) regret or harmful-intervention bounds.

### Experimental setup

- **Environments:** repeated matrix coordination and social-dilemma games; MPE; OvercookedV2; optionally a small SMACv2 subset.
- **Agents:** 2-5; focal Bayesian/source-aware agent; peer Q-learners, policy-gradient learners, aspiration learners, stationary policies, and response learners.
- **Observations/actions:** standard local observations/actions; source and peer parameters hidden.
- **Communication:** none in the minimal theorem; controlled messages as an extension.
- **Baselines:** stationary response, sliding window/change point, LeMOL/MBOM-style model, SOM, robust/minimax policy, opponent shaping, source oracle.
- **Metrics:** source calibration, interventional log loss, team return, dynamic regret, exploitability, convention welfare, harmful-shaping rate, intervention cost.
- **Compute estimate:** CPU for exact games; one modern GPU for 1-3 days for Overcooked/MPE sweeps. No proprietary model is required.

### Expected result

The strongest supporting result would be a proved passive-observation impossibility, an intervention condition that resolves it, and consistent held-out control improvement under equal intervention budgets.

### Falsification

Reject the direction if ordinary change-point detection or predictive opponent modeling selects the correct response at equal cost, if interventions add no held-out value, or if improvement persists after source labels are permuted.

### Ablations

- passive only versus intervention;
- matched versus unmatched passive distributions;
- source posterior versus peer-policy posterior only;
- correct, permuted, and oracle source labels;
- safe versus unconstrained probing;
- held-out peer update rules and hyperparameters;
- exogenous drift matched in magnitude and timing.

### Main reviewer objection

“This is ordinary system identification with a renamed latent variable.”

### Defense

Exhibit two passive-observation-equivalent multi-agent games requiring opposite actions because only one peer responds causally to the ego. If that construction cannot be made, the project should be killed.

### Assessment

- **Novelty confidence:** Medium
- **AAMAS main-track potential:** High
- **Feasibility:** High
- **Risk:** Medium
- **Overall weighted score:** 8.80/10

## Deep Dive: Research Direction 2

### One-line idea

Turn offline ad-hoc teamwork from “train and hope” into a certified decision: act only when logged joint behavior identifies safe coordination with the deployment teammate, otherwise buy minimal interaction.

### The research gap

Offline AHT methods learn from fixed logs and report generalization to unseen teammates. They do not generally characterize when an unseen-teammate value or best response is identifiable from those logs. Marginal state-action coverage by the focal agent can be high while partner-conditioned joint reachability is absent.

### Why existing methods fail

Sequence models and conservative offline objectives can still extrapolate across a change in partner policy. OOD scores do not directly measure whether the missing joint histories change the optimal coordination action. Safety guarantees that assume a supplied teammate-policy class do not establish whether the logged data identify the relevant class.

### Closest prior work

- [TAGET: Offline Goal-Based Decision Transformers for AHT](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhang25h.html)
- [ComaDICE](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/477d1531c260f9605bbf9d14d00952d4-Abstract-Conference.html)
- [DiTAC](https://doi.org/10.3233/FAIA251257)
- [Seldonian Reinforcement Learning for AHT](https://rlj.cs.umass.edu/2025/papers/Paper223.html)
- [Minimum Coverage Sets for Robust AHT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09595)
- [Controlling Type Confounding in AHT](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/xing23a.html)

### Why this is different

The contribution is not another offline policy. It is a teammate-conditioned indistinguishability result, a sufficient joint-coverage condition, a calibrated ambiguity certificate, and an explicit decision to act, abstain, or request a small number of probes.

### Research question

When does a fixed dataset from several team compositions identify a policy for a new teammate, and what is the minimum online interaction required when it does not?

### Falsifiable hypothesis

A teammate-conditioned ambiguity certificate will dominate standard density/OOD and single-agent concentrability diagnostics on selective risk-coverage and total utility, especially in cases with identical focal-agent marginals but different partner-conditioned reachability.

### Proposed approach

1. Define an equivalence class of POSG/team models consistent with the logged data.
2. Compute exact ambiguity width for small games.
3. Derive a sufficient teammate-conditional coverage/concentrability condition.
4. Learn a conservative proxy that predicts ambiguity width.
5. Select among act, abstain, or information-valued probe under explicit cost.

### Formalization

Let (D) be trajectories from a mixture of behavior joint policies and let the deployment teammate policy be (pi^{-i}_{test}). Define value ambiguity as the range of (V(pi^i,pi^{-i}_{test})) across all POSG/team models consistent with (D). Prove that equal focal-agent marginals need not imply equal value under a new teammate, and characterize when joint occupancy ratios bound the ambiguity.

### Experimental setup

- **Environments:** tabular coordination games, LBF, OvercookedV2, and a small Hanabi setting.
- **Agents:** one focal offline learner plus held-out partner populations trained with different algorithms/seeds.
- **Data:** deliberately vary unilateral coverage, joint coverage, teammate diversity, and confounding.
- **Baselines:** behavior cloning, conservative offline RL, TAGET, ComaDICE, DiTAC where available, density/OOD scores, always-act, always-abstain, online AHT oracle.
- **Metrics:** worst-partner return, selective risk-coverage, failure calibration, probe count/cost, regret to teammate-aware oracle.
- **Compute estimate:** tabular work on CPU; one GPU for 2-4 days for partner-pool evaluation. Public Overcooked/JaxMARL tooling exists.

### Expected result

Support requires a counterexample unique to joint teammate-conditioned coverage, an exact condition that predicts failure, and a learned certificate that retains useful coverage while reducing catastrophic coordination errors.

### Falsification

Reject if standard state-action support or Minimum Coverage Sets already predicts failure equally well, if the proposed term is algebraically implied by ordinary offline-RL concentrability, or if abstention/probing does not improve total cost-adjusted utility.

### Ablations

- focal marginal versus joint occupancy coverage;
- fixed versus held-out partner algorithms;
- exact ambiguity width versus learned proxy;
- no probe, random probe, information-valued probe, oracle probe;
- partial versus full observability;
- constructed impossibility cases versus independently trained partner pools.

### Main reviewer objection

“Offline-RL coverage theory and Minimum Coverage Sets already say this.”

### Defense

The paper must prove a joint-policy counterexample with identical focal-agent coverage but different deployment value. The red-team recommends rejecting this direction if no genuinely multi-agent term survives that comparison.

### Assessment

- **Novelty confidence:** Medium; one auditor rated it medium-high relative, the red-team would reject without the joint-support theorem
- **AAMAS main-track potential:** High
- **Feasibility:** High
- **Risk:** Medium-high novelty risk
- **Overall weighted score:** 8.55/10

## Deep Dive: Research Direction 3

### One-line idea

Model AHT probing as dual control when the probe teaches or displaces the teammate, so the target of inference changes because it was measured.

### The research gap

Team probing selects actions to identify a teammate. Opponent shaping selects actions to influence a learner. The residual is uncertainty over the teammate's learning rule, where each probe has task value, information value, and causal policy-displacement cost.

### Why existing methods fail

Stationary-type inference becomes misspecified when a diagnostic action anchors a convention or changes learning dynamics. Known-learner shaping can exploit the wrong response model. Immediate best response can create self-confirming, inefficient conventions.

### Closest prior work

- [Learning to Coordinate With Different Teammates via Team Probing](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10988880)
- [Meta-MAPG](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/kim21g.html)
- [Teaching and Leading an Ad Hoc Teammate](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.artint.2013.07.003)
- [Influencing Towards Stable Multi-Agent Interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08229)
- [Fast Teammate Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05911)
- [N-Agent Ad Hoc Teamwork](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10740)
- [ICRL4AHT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24423)

### Why this is different

The latent state includes both teammate policy and update-rule type. The algorithm values information while explicitly predicting how the same action changes the peer. Neither “probe a stable teammate” nor “shape a known learner” solves that belief-influence trade-off.

### Research question

How should an ad-hoc agent choose diagnostic actions when those actions update the unfamiliar teammate's policy, and what identifiability/regret guarantees are possible over restricted learning-rule families?

### Falsifiable hypothesis

An influence-aware Bayesian probe will reduce convention lock-in and regret relative to stationary-type probing and known-learner shaping on held-out update rules, without reducing partner welfare under a Pareto constraint.

### Proposed approach

Maintain a belief over partner policy and learning rule; predict both observation information and partner displacement for candidate probes; plan over a short horizon using a partner-welfare or Pareto constraint; fall back to robust actions when learning rules are observationally equivalent.

### Formalization

Use a Bayesian POSG whose latent state contains teammate parameter (theta_t) and rule (rho), with (theta_{t+1}=F_rho(theta_t,h_t,a^i_t,eta_t)). The value of action (a) includes task reward, expected posterior reduction, and a welfare-weighted displacement term. Theory should characterize equivalence classes of update rules and Bayesian regret in a finite rule family.

### Experimental setup

- **Environments:** iterated coordination matrices, OvercookedV2, LBF.
- **Partners:** stationary, Q-learning, policy-gradient, aspiration, bounded-memory, and response learners; algorithms/hyperparameters held out.
- **Baselines:** no probing, stationary Bayes probing, Team Probing/CDP, Meta-MAPG, known-learner shaping, robust/minimax AHT, rule oracle.
- **Metrics:** team and partner return, rule calibration, convention efficiency, partner-policy displacement, recovery after rule switch, regret.
- **Compute estimate:** CPU for formal games; one GPU for 2-4 days for public MARL environments.

### Expected result

The method should select different probes for observationally similar stationary and responsive teammates, infer useful rule equivalence classes, and improve held-out joint welfare under equal interaction budgets.

### Falsification

Reject if matched exogenous drift produces the same benefit, stationary probing matches performance on held-out learning rules, or gains disappear under equalized interaction and compute.

### Ablations

- policy belief only versus policy-plus-rule belief;
- known versus unknown rule;
- information term versus displacement term;
- ego-only versus Pareto-constrained objective;
- matched exogenous drift;
- held-out learning algorithm and hyperparameter.

### Main reviewer objection

“This is Team Probing plus opponent shaping.”

### Defense

The paper must prove or empirically isolate a belief-influence trade-off that neither baseline represents. If the method is just an opponent model added to the probing loss, reject it.

### Assessment

- **Novelty confidence:** Medium
- **AAMAS main-track potential:** High
- **Feasibility:** High
- **Risk:** Medium
- **Overall weighted score:** 8.45/10

## Deep Dive: Research Direction 4

### One-line idea

Track a translation between independently learned protocols when both sides keep changing online and neither has a shared language, parallel corpus, or fixed grounding anchor.

### The research gap

Translation and community-joining methods usually freeze the target protocol or use offline interactions/shared grounding. Drift and repair methods generally assume a stable semantics that can be certified or renegotiated. The narrow residual is **coupled two-sided drift**, where translation errors affect both learners and may cause oscillation or semantic collapse.

### Why existing methods fail

A translator trained at time (t) becomes stale when both encoder-decoder pairs update. If agents adapt to translation errors, ordinary online domain adaptation changes the very protocols it is tracking. A shared natural-language anchor or public stimulus would remove the hardest identifiability issue.

### Closest prior work

- [Emergent Translation in Multi-Agent Communication](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06922)
- [Learning Translations / CLAP](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/0005)
- [Unsupervised Translation of Emergent Communication](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/34489)
- [LangGround](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=LGsed0QQVq)
- [Implicit Repair with RL in Emergent Communication](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2025/pdfs/p2115.pdf)
- [Talk is Cheap, Communication is Hard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01750)
- [Verifiable Semantics for Agent-to-Agent Communication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16424)

### Why this is different

CLAP learns into a sufficiently stable target community; emergent translation uses shared visual grounding; unsupervised translation is not a coupled online control problem. The proposal removes those anchors and asks for tracking/stability limits while both protocols adapt because of joint outcomes.

### Research question

At what relative drift rates and under what excitation conditions can decentralized agents track two changing protocol maps from task outcomes alone, and when is collapse unavoidable?

### Falsifiable hypothesis

Timescale-separated translator updates with active disambiguating interactions will achieve sublinear tracking regret below a drift threshold, while naive simultaneous adaptation will undergo a measurable transition to oscillation or semantic collapse.

### Proposed approach

Model protocol states as time-varying latent permutations or compositional maps; use outcome-based cycle consistency and change-point-aware timescale separation; select disambiguating task interactions; trigger renegotiation only when tracking uncertainty exceeds a bound.

### Formalization

In a repeated signaling game, each agent has encoder (E_i^t) and decoder (D_i^t). The translator observes messages, local histories, and joint outcomes but no paired meaning labels. Bound dynamic regret to an oracle translator in terms of total variation of both protocol paths, excitation, and asynchronous update rates. Prove non-identifiability without task variation or symmetry breaking.

### Experimental setup

- **Environments:** Lewis/referential games, cooperative navigation, Hanabi-like signaling, and one heterogeneous sensor/action task.
- **Agents:** 2-8 independently trained populations with asynchronous online updates.
- **Communication:** discrete messages; no natural-language/shared-representation anchor in the primary setting.
- **Baselines:** fixed translator, CLAP-style adaptation, unsupervised translation, independent change detection, reset/renegotiation, oracle map.
- **Metrics:** cross-play return, semantic tracking error, dynamic regret, oscillation, recovery time, message cost, convention survival.
- **Compute estimate:** one GPU for 1-3 days for the core study; 3-7 days for population and architecture sweeps.

### Expected result

Support requires a stable operating region predicted by the formal bound and an empirical collapse boundary that shifts as the method predicts under held-out learners and drift directions.

### Falsification

Reject if a fixed/change-detected CLAP baseline matches the method, if a hidden common grounding signal is required, or if the phenomenon is fully explained by ordinary non-stationary classification.

### Ablations

- one-sided versus two-sided drift;
- shared grounding versus none;
- synchronous versus asynchronous updates;
- drift magnitude/direction and update timescale;
- active disambiguation versus passive outcomes;
- reset/renegotiation versus continuous tracking.

### Main reviewer objection

“This is non-stationary domain adaptation applied to messages.”

### Defense

Meaning must be relational and identifiable only through another adapting decision-maker's actions and outcomes. If the problem can be solved from independently labeled message streams, it should not be submitted as MAS research.

### Assessment

- **Novelty confidence:** Medium; medium-high relative confidence in the novelty audit, reduced by the red-team because translation and repair are separately crowded
- **AAMAS main-track potential:** Medium-high
- **Feasibility:** High
- **Risk:** Medium
- **Overall weighted score:** 8.38/10

## Deep Dive: Research Direction 5

### One-line idea

Characterize what can be learned and truthfully elicited when complementary assignments alter private capability, agents can withhold effort, and only coalition success is visible.

### The research gap

Task allocation methods handle unknown/evolving capabilities cooperatively. Mechanism-design work handles private execution types with transfers or more direct outcome information. Coalition bandits handle aggregate reward but not strategic effort and action-dependent capability. Their conjunction creates a specific identification-incentive obstruction.

### Why existing methods fail

Failure of a coalition can reflect missing complementarity, low capability, fatigue caused by prior assignments, or strategic under-effort. Self-reports are manipulable, while pure outcome inference can punish agents assigned harder work. Without carefully chosen diagnostic coalitions, individual contributions may be unidentifiable.

### Why this is different

The residual requires all load-bearing conditions: complementary tasks, private action-dependent capability, strategic report/effort, coalition-only outcomes, repeated interaction, and no monetary transfers. Removing any one returns to a well-developed neighboring model.

### Research question

What identifiability and approximate incentive guarantees are possible for repeated payment-free allocation when capabilities evolve because of assignments and only coalition outcomes are observed?

### Falsifiable hypothesis

Under an explicit rank/connectivity condition on diagnostic coalitions and bounded capability drift, rotation/audit assignments can achieve lower welfare regret and manipulation gain than cooperative capability inference or static truthful-report mechanisms; outside that condition, capability and effort are provably indistinguishable.

### Proposed approach

Start with binary complementary skills and one Markov fatigue/learning model. Prove an impossibility without diagnostic variation. Design a repeated allocator that uses difficulty-normalized diagnostic coalitions and future access/assignment incentives rather than money. Expand to a robot simulator only after the exact model works.

### Formalization

Use a dynamic combinatorial bandit/principal-multi-agent game. Each agent has hidden capability state (c_{i,t}), hidden effort (e_{i,t}), and report (r_{i,t}). Assignment changes (c_{i,t+1}); only coalition outcome (y_t=f(S_t,c_t,e_t)+noise) is observed. Targets are rank-based identifiability, welfare regret, and approximate incentive compatibility or an impossibility boundary.

### Experimental setup

- **Environment:** synthetic complementary-skill tasks plus one public heterogeneous pickup/inspection simulator.
- **Agents:** 5-30 utility-maximizing scripted/learned agents; no reliance on prompted LLM self-interest.
- **Actions:** reports, effort, and task/coalition assignment.
- **Baselines:** combinatorial UCB, cooperative adaptive MRTA, CASH oracle descriptors, Shapley credit, fixed-type truthful-report mechanism, static/drift oracle.
- **Metrics:** welfare and allocation regret, capability calibration, manipulation gain, diagnostic cost, workload/fairness, catastrophic failure.
- **Compute estimate:** CPU for exact/synthetic games; one GPU for 2-5 days for simulator sweeps.

### Expected result

The ideal result is a sharp impossibility followed by minimal sufficient assumptions and an allocator whose welfare/incentive behavior follows the predicted coalition-design boundary.

### Falsification

Reject if standard combinatorial-bandit or contract methods achieve the same regret/incentive guarantees, coalition recombination adds no identification value, or the mechanism requires assumptions that eliminate strategic behavior.

### Ablations

- fixed versus evolving capability;
- capability drift caused by allocation versus exogenous drift;
- individual versus coalition-only feedback;
- transfers, bounded non-monetary incentives, and none;
- truthful, misreporting, and under-effort agents;
- diagnostic-coalition rank/connectivity;
- known versus misspecified transition model.

### Main reviewer objection

“This is four papers' worth of complications.”

### Defense

Restrict the first paper to binary complementary skills, one drift model, coalition-only feedback, and no transfers. The central result must be one identifiability/impossibility boundary plus one matching algorithm, not a universal task allocator.

## Recommended Direction

### Causal source attribution for multi-agent non-stationarity

This is the strongest overall opportunity.

1. **Why it is novel enough to investigate:** searched work predicts or disentangles changing peers and context, but we found no direct treatment of the three-way interventional attribution problem with matched passive traces and control consequences.
2. **Why it matters:** tracking, robustness, and shaping are qualitatively different responses. Misidentifying the source can destabilize cooperation or manipulate a teammate needlessly.
3. **Why AAMAS fits:** the distinguishing causal edge is another agent's strategic or learning response to the focal agent. Removing that agent changes the problem, not merely the implementation.
4. **Why it is feasible:** the decisive theorem and first experiment can be done in small games; public MPE and Overcooked environments support transfer without proprietary models or large compute.
5. **Core scientific contribution:** an observational non-identifiability result, sufficient bounded-intervention conditions, and a source-conditioned controller with regret or harmful-shaping guarantees.
6. **Required experiments:** passive-equivalent process pairs; exact small-game validation; held-out learner families; matched intervention budgets; comparison with change detection, causal opponent models, robust control, and shaping.
7. **What could make it fail:** existing causal-game theory may subsume the construction; the three processes may be separable by ordinary prediction; or correct attribution may not change control performance.

The recommended first action is not implementation. It is to construct the smallest passive-equivalent pair and prove that their optimal interventions differ. Failure to do that should terminate the project cheaply.

## Research Plan

### Phase 1 — Literature verification

- Re-run exact searches for causal influence identification, reflexive system identification, endogenous versus exogenous non-stationarity, causal opponent modeling, adaptive control with strategic responders, and dual control in AHT.
- Backward- and forward-chain the closest seven papers.
- Ask one mechanism-design/game-theory specialist and one AHT specialist whether the two-world construction is known under different terminology.
- **Exit gate:** a written closest-work matrix and no theorem that subsumes the formulation by relabeling.

### Phase 2 — Problem formalization

- Define the three structural process classes and the allowed local history.
- Construct passive-observation-equivalent instances.
- Prove non-identifiability without interventions and a minimal sufficient intervention condition.
- Define harmful shaping and dynamic regret.
- **Exit gate:** a nontrivial proposition or counterexample that cannot be reduced to a stationary POMDP.

### Phase 3 — Baseline implementation

- Implement exact small games and source oracle.
- Add fixed-window/change-point, LeMOL/MBOM-style predictor, robust, and shaping baselines.
- Match interaction and intervention budgets.
- **Exit gate:** all baselines recover their expected behavior on diagnostic instances.

### Phase 4 — Prototype method

- Implement posterior inference over structural source models.
- Add safe expected-value-of-information intervention selection.
- Condition track/hedge/shape control on uncertainty.
- **Exit gate:** source-aware decisions beat label-permuted controls on held-out exact games.

### Phase 5 — Experiments

- Run repeated games, MPE, and OvercookedV2.
- Hold out peer algorithms and hyperparameters, not only seeds.
- Report source calibration, regret, welfare, harmful shaping, and intervention cost with confidence intervals.
- **Exit gate:** benefit survives matched exploration and exogenous-drift controls.

### Phase 6 — Ablations

- Remove the source variable, intervention planner, and safety constraint separately.
- Permute source labels.
- Match passive distributions and intervention counts.
- Test misspecified source families and unseen peers.
- **Exit gate:** the claimed mechanism, not extra exploration or compute, explains the gain.

### Phase 7 — Paper positioning

- Lead with the information obstruction and control consequence.
- Treat LLM agents, if used, as one transfer population—not the definition of the problem.
- Position against causal opponent modeling, non-stationary MARL, opponent shaping, dual control, and AHT.
- Avoid “first,” “nobody,” and broad claims that multi-agent non-stationarity is unstudied.
